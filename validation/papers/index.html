    The two papers side by side¶

    Do the two MARLEM papers describe the same method? This page compares every equation and design statement that appears in both, says where they agree and where they differ, and which of the two the rebuild follows. It is a first pass on the method only; the second paper's results are not reviewed here.

    The papers and how they divide the work¶

    [P1] [P2]
    Title MARLEM: a multi-agent reinforcement learning simulation framework for implicit cooperation in decentralized local energy markets (Applied Energy 412, 2026, 127546) Harnessing implicit cooperation: a multi-agent reinforcement learning approach towards decentralized local energy markets (SSRN preprint, not peer reviewed)
    Authors Salazar-Peña, Tabares, González-Mancera the same
    Role The framework: market, DSO, agents, batteries, grid, KPIs, reward, paradigms (overview only) The MARL study: implicit cooperation, the three paradigms and three algorithms in detail, the 3 × 3 experiment
    Equations (1)-(27) (1)-(20)
    Case study Batteries, zero-intelligence agents (§4) 8 agents on IEEE 34, 3 × 3 MARL matrix (§4)

    Each paper hands part of the method to the other:

    • [P2] defers the environment to [P1]. "While the complete mathematical derivation of the environment is detailed in [9], we focus here on the specific augmentations that drive cooperative behavior" ([P2] §3.1; [9] is [P1]). [P2] restates no market, DSO, agent, battery or loss equation.
    • [P1] defers the MARL method to [P2]. "A detailed treatment of the MARL framework, including its full mathematical and implementation specifics, is reserved for a forthcoming paper" ([P1] §3.7).

    So the two papers overlap in only five places, and only those can disagree:

    1. the Dec-POMDP and the observation;
    2. the grid balance and grid congestion;
    3. the reward;
    4. the KPIs;
    5. the training paradigms and algorithms.

    For the market layer this means [P1] is the only specification. Everything in the market validation is checked against [P1] alone; [P2] neither confirms nor contradicts it.

    Four real disagreements, all in the reward, where the original code is a third version (§3 shows it next to both papers). Everything else either agrees or is written in only one of the papers.

    1. Dec-POMDP and observation¶

    Agree. Both formulate the market as a Dec-POMDP ⟨I, S, A, T, R, Ω, O⟩ in which each agent sees a local observation of three parts: market signals (public), agent-specific signals (private) and implicit-cooperation KPIs (shared). The paragraphs describing the three parts are the same text in both papers, and [P1] Table 2 and [P2] Table 5 list the same 42 signals in the same order:

    Market signals (16) Agent signals (16) KPIs (10)
    step, time of day, clearing price and volume, grid balance, DSO buy/sell/total volume, P2P volume, DSO trade ratio, net grid import, DSO buy/sell price, mean local price, price spread, local price advantage generation, demand, cumulative demand satisfied/deferred, remaining demand, cumulative supply satisfied/deferred, remaining supply, mean profit, reputation, battery energy, SoC, available charge/discharge, cumulative charge/discharge social welfare, liquidity, mean bid-ask spread, price volatility, supply-demand imbalance, grid congestion, coordination score, coordination convergence, DER self-consumption, flexibility utilization

    Small differences in wording only:

    • [P1] adds the state factorisation, eq. 1, \(s_t = \langle s^{\text{time}}, s^{\text{market}}, s^{\text{agents}}, s^{\text{DSO}}, s^{\text{grid}}, s^{\text{history}} \rangle\); [P2] does not repeat it.
    • [P2] §3.1 says agents "observe the P2P trade ratio". The table has the DSO trade ratio, which is its complement, so the information is the same.
    • [P2] Table 7 gives the forecast error (30 %) that [P1] mentions only as "forecasts".

    The action \(a_i = \langle p_{\text{bid}}, q_{\text{bid}}, \alpha, \beta \rangle\) ([P1] eq. 2) is not restated in [P2].

    2. Grid balance and grid congestion¶

    Grid balance: same formula, different description.

    \[ \text{[P1] (20), [P2] (1):}\qquad B_{\text{grid}} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \left( G_i - D_i + E_{\text{bought},i} - E_{\text{sold},i} \right) \]
    • [P1] describes it as "the sum of all agents' net energy positions": close to 0 means a self-sufficient market, large values mean heavy reliance on the external grid.
    • [P2] calls it "a normalized indicator of the net energy position", with positive meaning surplus. Eq. 1 has no normalisation, and neither paper says what it would be normalised by.

    Both papers also list "grid balance" as a KPI, described as "the difference between total generation and consumption". That is \(\sum (G - D)\) only, without the trade terms of the equation they cite.

    Grid congestion: agree. [P1] eq. 19 defines it per line, \(\zeta_e = F_e / \max(C_e)\), and the KPI as "the average congestion level across all power lines as defined in (19)". [P2] eq. 2 writes that average out:

    \[ \bar{\zeta} = \frac{1}{\lvert \xi \rvert} \sum_{e \in \xi} \frac{F_e}{\max(C_e)} \]

    3. Reward¶

    This is where the papers disagree, and the original code is a third version. Each part below shows [P1], [P2] and the code. The code is copied from src/environment/reward.py in the original repository, salazarna/marlem (commit 15d6715).

    Overall form: three versions¶

    \[ \text{[P1] (3):}\qquad R_i = \operatorname{sign}(r_{\text{raw}}) \cdot \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{\lvert r_{\text{raw}} \rvert}{20}\right) \cdot 20 \]
    \[ \text{[P1] (4):}\qquad r_{\text{raw}} = R_{\text{base},i} \cdot \left(1 + \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{\max(0,\ f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib},i} - 1)}{5}\right)\right) - \gamma_{\text{DSO},i} - \gamma_{\text{UD},i} \]
    \[ \text{[P2] (3):}\qquad R_i = R_{\text{base},i} \cdot \left(1 + f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib},i}\right) - \gamma_{\text{DSO},i} - \gamma_{\text{UD},i} \]

    The code (reward.py L228-L242):

    cooperation_boost = (1.0 + cooperation_factor * contribution_factor)

boosted_base_reward = base_reward * (1.0 + np.log1p(max(0.0, cooperation_boost - 1.0)) / 5.0)

penalty_scale = max(max(abs(boosted_base_reward), 1.0), 1.0) * 0.3
normalized_dso_penalty = dso_penalty * penalty_scale
normalized_demand_penalty = demand_response_penalty * penalty_scale / self.p_dso

raw_reward = boosted_base_reward - normalized_dso_penalty - normalized_demand_penalty

return np.sign(raw_reward) * np.log1p(np.abs(raw_reward) / 20.0) * 20.0

    Written as an equation, the code is:

    \[ \text{code:}\qquad r_{\text{raw}} = R_{\text{base},i} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\ln\!\left(1 + f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib},i}\right)}{5}\right) - s \cdot \gamma_{\text{DSO},i} - s \cdot \frac{\gamma_{\text{UD},i}}{p_{\text{dso}}}, \qquad s = 0.3 \cdot \max(\lvert R_{\text{base},i} \cdot (\ldots) \rvert, 1) \]
    [P1] eq. 3-4 [P2] eq. 3 Code
    Final log compression Yes, scale 20 None Yes, scale 20 (as [P1] eq. 3)
    Cooperation bonus on \(R_{\text{base}}\) \(\ln\left(1 + \max(0,\ f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}} - 1) / 5\right)\) \(f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}}\) \(\ln(1 + f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}}) / 5\)
    Bonus at \(f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}} = 1\) (its maximum) 0 1 (doubles \(R_{\text{base}}\)) \(\ln 2 / 5 \approx 0.14\)
    Penalties scaled by the reward's size Said in the text ("implicitly scaled relative to the reward magnitude"), not in the equation No Yes, by \(s\)

    What this shows.

    • [P1] eq. 4 is the code, mistranscribed. In the code, max(0, cooperation_boost − 1) subtracts the 1 that cooperation_boost itself added, so it is just \(f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}}\). [P1] printed \(\max(0,\ f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}} - 1)\), dropping the \(1 +\), and moved the \(/\,5\) inside the logarithm.
    • The printed version is inert. Both papers say \(f_{\text{coop}}\) and \(f_{\text{contrib}}\) lie in [0, 1], so their product is at most 1. Under [P1] eq. 4 the bonus is then always 0, and the reward is just \(R_{\text{base}}\) minus the penalties.
    • The code's bonus is small on paper but large in practice. With both factors in [0, 1] it reaches at most 14 % of \(R_{\text{base}}\). But the code's \(f_{\text{coop}}\) is not in [0, 1]: it is about 1811 in a traced episode (register I-41, below). With \(f_{\text{contrib}} = 0.5\) the bonus is then \(\ln(1 + 906) / 5 \approx 1.36\), which more than doubles \(R_{\text{base}}\).
    • [P2] states a far stronger bonus, up to 100 % of \(R_{\text{base}}\), and calls it "the core contribution of this work regarding implicit cooperation". [P2]'s experiments ran on the code (register I-42), so the bonus the trained agents saw was the code's, not [P2]'s.

    Contribution factor \(f_{\text{contrib}}\): [P1] matches the code, [P2] does not¶

    [P1] eq. 6 [P2] §3.1
    Formula \(0.5\, r_{\text{grid}} + 0.3\, r_{\text{share}} + 0.2\, r_{\text{dev}}\) "a weighted sum of the agent's marginal contribution to reducing grid imbalance (\(-\partial \lvert B_{\text{grid}} \rvert / \partial a_i\)), its contribution to price efficiency, and its contribution to local trading volume" (no weights)
    Grid part \(r_{\text{grid}}\): a score, 0.5 for neutral actions, toward 1 if the agent's trade reduces the imbalance, toward 0 if it worsens it The marginal effect of the agent's action on \(\lvert B_{\text{grid}} \rvert\)

    The code (reward.py L487-L514):

    grid_contribution = NEUTRAL                    # 0.5

if matching.grid_balance != 0:
    net_contribution = sum(t.quantity if t.seller_id == agent.id else -t.quantity for t in agent_trades)
    if (matching.grid_balance > 0 and net_contribution < 0) or (matching.grid_balance < 0 and net_contribution > 0):
        impact_ratio = min(1.0, abs(net_contribution) / abs(matching.grid_balance))
        grid_contribution = 0.5 + (0.5 * impact_ratio)   # [0.5-1.0] range for helpful actions
    else:
        impact_ratio = min(1.0, abs(net_contribution) / abs(matching.grid_balance))
        grid_contribution = 0.5 - (0.5 * impact_ratio)   # [0.0-0.5] range for unhelpful actions

# ...
    price_contribution = max(0.0, min(1.0, 1.0 - avg_price_deviation * 2))

local_contribution = len(agent_local_trades) / len(agent_trades) if agent_trades else 0

return (self._w_grid * grid_contribution) + (self._w_price * price_contribution) + (self._w_local * local_contribution)

    with _w_grid = 0.5, _w_price = 0.2, _w_local = 0.3 (L92-L95).

    What this shows.

    • [P1] eq. 6 describes this code exactly: the weights 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2, the 0.5 neutral point, the share of P2P trades, the price deviation.
    • [P2]'s marginal contribution \(-\partial \lvert B_{\text{grid}} \rvert / \partial a_i\) exists nowhere. The code scores only whether the agent's net trade has the right sign and how large it is against the balance (register I-79). Neither paper shows how a derivative with respect to an action could be taken in an auction-cleared market.
    • The grid part is constant in practice. It reads matching.grid_balance, which is the DSO balance and always 0 (register I-24). The if never runs, so the grid part is always 0.5 (register I-44).

    DSO penalty \(\gamma_{\text{DSO}}\): [P2] matches the code, [P1] does not¶

    • [P1]: "penalties for reliance on the DSO (\(p_{\text{dso}} = 15.0\)) and unmet demand (\(p_{\text{no_trades}} = 5.0\))". The penalties are fixed.
    • [P2]: \(\gamma_{\text{DSO}}\) "is applied for trading with the DSO, scaled by the current grid imbalance … amplified during critical imbalance periods".

    The code (reward.py L207-L223, L138-L139):

    # γ_DSO
dso_ratio = dso_quantity / total_quantity if total_quantity > 0 else 0.0
dso_penalty = dso_ratio * self.p_dso * abs(self.dso.balance) / max(self.grid_network.capacity, 1.0)

# γ_UD
if is_terminal:
    unmet_demand = max(0.0, agent.total_demand_required - agent.cumulative_demand_satisfied)
    if agent.total_demand_required > 0:
        unmet_demand_ratio = unmet_demand / agent.total_demand_required
        demand_response_penalty = self.p_dso * (unmet_demand_ratio ** 2)

# where p_no_trades is actually used: an agent without trades gets a fixed reward
if not agent_trades:
    return np.sign(-self.p_no_trades) * np.log1p(np.abs(-self.p_no_trades) / 20.0) * 20.0

    What this shows.

    • \(\gamma_{\text{DSO}}\): [P2]'s description is the code's. The penalty is the agent's DSO share \(\times\ p_{\text{dso}} \times \lvert \text{imbalance} \rvert / \text{capacity}\). [P1]'s "\(p_{\text{dso}} = 15.0\)" is only the factor inside it.
    • In practice there is no DSO penalty. The imbalance used is dso.balance, always 0 (register I-24), so the penalty is always 0.
    • \(\gamma_{\text{UD}}\) uses \(p_{\text{dso}}\), not [P1]'s \(p_{\text{no_trades}} = 5\) (register I-46). It runs only when is_terminal is true, which never happens (register I-02).
    • \(p_{\text{no_trades}}\) does something else: it is the fixed reward −4.46 returned to an agent that made no trade at all, before any other term (register I-47).

    Trading reward: [P1] matches the code's accident, [P2] its intent¶

    • [P1]: "Trading success (\(w_{\text{trade}} = 5.0\)): … defined as \(0.3\,\rho_{\text{local}} + 0.3\,\rho_{\text{dso}}\)". Local and DSO fills weigh the same.
    • [P2]: "a trading component which incentivizes successful transactions with a higher weight for P2P transactions to create a preference for local coordination".

    The code (reward.py L80-L95, L262):

    # Trade weights
self._w_local: float = 0.7  # Higher weight for local trades
self._w_dso: float = 0.3  # Lower weight for DSO trades
# ...
# Contribution weights
self._w_grid: float = 0.5  # Grid contribution
self._w_price: float = 0.2  # Price contribution
self._w_local: float = 0.3  # Local trading contribution

# _calculate_trading_reward
return (self._w_local * local_quantity_ratio) + (self._w_dso * dso_quantity_ratio)

    What this shows. One attribute name, _w_local, is used for two different weights. The trading reward's local weight (0.7, "higher weight for local trades") is overwritten fifteen lines later by \(f_{\text{contrib}}\)'s local weight (0.3). The trading reward therefore runs as 0.3 / 0.3 (register I-45).

    • [P1] printed the result of the accident, 0.3 / 0.3.
    • [P2] describes the intent, a higher P2P weight, which is what the code's first assignment and its comment say.
    • The weights in the code are right for \(f_{\text{contrib}}\), which matches [P1] eq. 6 (above).

    \(R_{\text{base}}\) and \(f_{\text{coop}}\): agree, numbers only in [P1]¶

    Both list the same five base components: economic efficiency, grid balance, resource allocation, trading and stability. Only [P1] gives weights (10, 1.5, 5, 2, 3) and ranges, and its weights match the code's.

    Both describe \(f_{\text{coop}}\) as a weighted sum of normalised system KPIs. Only [P1] gives the formula and weights (eq. 5, total weight 56). The code (L176-L198) computes it as [P1] eq. 5 but feeds it raw, unnormalised KPIs, so \(f_{\text{coop}}\) is nowhere near [0, 1]: about 1811 in a traced episode (register I-41, I-75). Neither paper gives the normalisation ranges.

    4. KPIs¶

    Agree. [P2] §3.2 repeats [P1] §3.6.2 nearly word for word, with the same equations under new numbers:

    KPI [P1] [P2] Formula
    Social welfare (21) (4) \(\sum_{\text{trades}} p \cdot q\)
    Market liquidity (22) (5) \(\sum_{\text{trades}} q\)
    Mean bid-ask spread (23) (6) \(\mathbb{E}[p_{\text{ask}}] - \mathbb{E}[p_{\text{bid}}]\)
    Price volatility text text std of the clearing price over a window
    Supply-demand imbalance (24) (7) \(\lvert \sum q_{\text{buy}} - \sum q_{\text{sell}} \rvert / C_{\text{grid}}\)
    Grid congestion (19) (2) mean line loading
    Grid balance (20) (1) as §2
    DER self-consumption (25) (8) \(\sum q_{\text{P2P}} / \sum (q_{\text{P2P}} + q_{\text{DSO}})\)
    Flexibility utilization (26) (9) \(\sum q_{\text{P2P}} / \sum q_{\text{available}}\)
    Coordination score (27) (10) \(1 - \text{Imbalance}\)
    Coordination convergence text text "calculated similarly to the price volatility"

    They agree, but they share the same problems, so [P2] does not resolve them:

    • Social welfare is described as "the sum of consumer and producer surplus" but written as \(\sum p \cdot q\), which is turnover. In true welfare, payments between agents cancel (register I-36).
    • Imbalance over trades is always 0, because every trade has one buyer and one seller. It means something only over orders or physical positions, and neither paper says which (register I-68). The coordination score, [P2]'s headline metric, is \(1 - \text{Imbalance}\).
    • Spread is \(\mathbb{E}[p_{\text{ask}}] - \mathbb{E}[p_{\text{bid}}]\), which is negative whenever bids exceed asks, that is, whenever there is anything to trade.
    • "DER self-consumption" measures the P2P share of traded energy, not self-consumption (register I-69).

    5. Training paradigms and algorithms¶

    Agree. [P1] describes CTCE, CTDE and DTDE in words; [P2] gives each a formula and says the same things:

    Paradigm [P1] §3.7.1 [P2] §3.4
    CTCE One policy, full global state, joint action; "a theoretical upper bound" The same, eq. 16 (joint policy) and eq. 17 (objective summed over all agents); "the theoretical upper bound for coordination quality"
    CTDE Centralized critic in training, local observation in execution The same, eq. 18 (actor gradient with a centralized critic Q(s, a))
    DTDE Independent learners; non-stationarity mitigated by the shared KPIs The same, eq. 19-20 (each agent's transition absorbs the others' policies)

    The same holds for the algorithms. [P1] §3.7.2 names PPO, APPO and SAC with one sentence each, and [P2] §3.3 gives their objectives (eq. 11-15). Both name Population-Based Training.

    One addition in [P2] matters for comparing the paradigms: eq. 17 sums the reward over all agents for CTCE, while CTDE and DTDE optimise each agent's own return (eq. 19). Returns of the three paradigms are therefore on different scales (register I-73). [P1] does not say how CTCE's reward is formed.

    6. Only in one paper¶

    Only in [P1] Only in [P2]
    The market: orders and validation (eq. 2, 15), three-stage clearing, pricing mechanisms (eq. 9-11) Algorithm objectives (eq. 11-15) and paradigm formulas (eq. 16-20)
    The DSO: balance (eq. 7), fees (eq. 8a-8h) The Grid Balance Index \(B_{\text{index}}\) (§4.3): "incorporates transmission losses and external grid reliance". Used as an evaluation metric, never defined, and absent from [P1] and from the code (register I-80)
    Reputation (eq. 12) and the ledger Agent responsiveness (§4.3): correlation between system signals and actions
    Agent balance and profit (eq. 13-14), battery (eq. 16), profiles (eq. 17)
    Losses (eq. 18), zones, the grid update
    Reward log-compression (eq. 3), all reward weights and penalty values

    Case setups (not a method difference)¶

    The two case studies differ, as expected for different experiments. The table is here only so that numbers from one are not read as the other's:

    [P1] §4 [P2] §4, Tables 6-7
    Agents 7, base capacity 60 kW 8, 75-180 kW
    Grid Mesh, 1200 kW IEEE 34-node, 1800 kW
    Agents' policy Zero intelligence (random bids) Trained (3 paradigms × 3 algorithms)
    Clearing mechanism Bid-ask spread Not stated
    Price range 35-280 $/MWh 20-600; DSO prices plotted in "$/kWh" (Fig. 3)
    Quantity range 0.1-200 kWh 0-180 kWh
    Batteries Ratio 0.5-1.2, \(\eta\) 95 % Ratio 0.5-1.0, \(\eta\) 95 %, SoC 5-95 %

    The price units differ by a factor of 1000 between the papers ($/MWh vs "$/kWh"), on top of the unit problems inside the code (register I-56).

    What the rebuild follows¶

    • Market layer (marlem-v1): [P1] alone, since [P2] restates none of it. Its gaps are listed in the market validation.
    • RL layer: the reward must be chosen, because the papers give two. The choices to record:
    • the overall form: [P1] eq. 4 (bonus inert as printed), the code's (what [P1] meant, \(\ln(1 + f_{\text{coop}} \cdot f_{\text{contrib}}) / 5\)) or [P2] eq. 3;
    • whether to keep [P1]'s log compression, eq. 3;
    • \(f_{\text{contrib}}\)'s grid part, a scored direction ([P1]) or a marginal effect ([P2]);
    • \(\gamma_{\text{DSO}}\), fixed or scaled by the imbalance;
    • the trading weights.

    The KPIs, observation and paradigms can follow either paper, since they agree.
