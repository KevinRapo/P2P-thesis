    RL layer: what is wrong and how it was tested¶

    Every problem found so far in the reinforcement-learning layer of MARLEM: in the original code, in the papers [P1] and [P2], or in the method itself. For each one, what is wrong, where it comes from, how it was found and, where a test exists, what the test showed. Nothing is fixed yet: this document records the state of the original. How the market and the RL layer connect (actions, observations, reward, KPIs) is left to a separate document.

    Scope and sources¶

    RL layer means everything that turns an environment into trained policies, independent of what the environment is:

    • the three training paradigms (CTCE, CTDE, DTDE) and their RL modules;
    • the grouping that turns all agents into one for CTCE and CTDE;
    • the algorithm settings (PPO, APPO, SAC) and the trainer around them (Ray Tune, population-based training, evaluation, checkpoints);
    • standalone inference of a trained policy.

    The market environment itself (lem.py), and what it gives the agents (observations, actions, reward, KPIs), is not in scope. To keep the two apart, the original RL code was copied out of the original repository unchanged and tested on small environments with known answers instead of the market (see The test bench). That copy, with its issue markers and the test notebooks, is kept in RL/ at commit bbef313 of the clean branch; the fixed layer is core/rl/ here, still independent of the market.

    Sources.

    • IDs are those of the issue register, the single list of every issue in the original; this page gives the detail for the RL layer. IDs R-xx are findings made on the RL test bench.
    • The RL folder (RL/ at commit bbef313 of the clean branch) holds the copied RL code, test environments, test bench and notebooks. Links below to the copied code and its notebooks point into it on GitHub, pinned to that commit.
    • The original code is the authors' MARLEM repository, salazarna/marlem, at commit 15d6715 (March 2026). Every code link and snippet points there, pinned to that commit. The RL files were copied unchanged into core/rl/ (commit bf58193), so the line numbers are the same in both.
    • [P1] is the MARLEM paper (Salazar-Peña et al., Applied Energy 412, 2026, 127546). It describes the paradigms in words (§3.7) and leaves MARL training to future work.
    • [P2] is the companion preprint (Harnessing implicit cooperation: a multi-agent reinforcement learning approach towards decentralized local energy markets, SSRN 6685504, not peer reviewed). It gives the paradigms' formulas (§3.4, eq. 16-20), the algorithms' objectives (§3.3, eq. 11-15) and trains the 3 × 3 matrix of paradigms and algorithms (§4-5).
    • RLlib is Ray 2.51.1, the version in the marlem conda env. Statements about RLlib's behaviour were checked in its installed source.

    The most consequential ones:

    • CTDE never trains the policies that act (I-48). The loss trains a central actor; the agents act with per-agent networks that stay at their random initial weights for any algorithm and any training length. Confirmed by test: the acting networks change by exactly 0.
    • CTCE and CTDE policies can diverge to NaN and keep "training" (R-01, R-02). Their action spread has no upper limit, so where the reward does not depend on the action the entropy bonus drives it to overflow. The KL term then becomes infinite, kl_coeff = 0 turns that into a NaN loss, and RLlib silently zeroes the NaN gradients and carries on. Confirmed by test.
    • SAC's actor in CTCE and CTDE never learns from the critic (R-05). It samples its action without the reparameterisation gradient, so it is trained only to raise its entropy.
    • The paradigms are not compared like for like (R-03, I-60, I-73). DTDE uses RLlib's standard network, CTCE and CTDE a smaller custom one without its safeguards; CTCE is trained on the sum of all rewards and learns more slowly.
    • Inference does not act like training (I-61). It skips RLlib's rescaling of actions to the action bounds and reads the market metrics from the wrong place.
    • Nothing in the original checks that anything learns (I-52, I-64). The trainer's test runs 5 iterations and asserts nothing, so I-48 passes it.

    How to read an issue¶

    Each issue below has the same parts:

    • Header: ID, title, origin, evidence.
    • Original code: the lines at fault, copied from the original repository (salazarna/marlem, commit 15d6715). The caption links to them on GitHub with their line numbers. # ... stands for lines cut for length.
    • Why it is wrong: what the lines do, which other code or RLlib behaviour makes it a problem, and how that differs from [P1] or [P2].
    • Test (evidence Tested): the hypothesis, the test that would show it, the result, and what it tells us.
    • How to test it (evidence Read): the test that would confirm or refute it.

    The test bench¶

    Validating RL code is harder than validating the market: a market rule can be checked by computing one step by hand, but a learning defect usually shows only as "the agents learn badly", which could also be the reward, the hyperparameters or too little training. The bench is built so that a failure has only one possible cause.

    Isolation. The RL code runs on small test environments instead of the market (core/rl/envs/). They have the same interface as the market environment (one Box observation and action per agent, per-agent rewards, episodes that end), so the code under test is exactly the original's.

    What is trusted. RLlib's algorithms themselves (the PPO, APPO and SAC losses, advantage estimation, the optimiser) are widely used and not re-validated. What is tested is MARLEM's own code around them: the grouping, the custom CTCE and CTDE modules, and the trainer's settings.

    Probe environments (probes.py), after Andy Jones, Debugging RL, Without the Agonizing Pain. Each probe has an exact correct answer and adds one piece of the learning problem to the previous one, so the first probe a paradigm fails names the broken piece. All have 3 agents, one-dimensional observations and actions in [-1, 1], and a reward per agent. For CTCE and CTDE the correct value is the sum over the agents, since their reward is the group's sum.

    Probe Observation Reward Correct answer Adds
    1 Constant always 0 +1, one step value 1 value learning, reward delivery, episode end
    2 Observation ±1, random per agent the agent's observation value = observation the observation reaches the value network
    3 Discount 0, then 1 +1 only at the end of 2 steps V(0) = γ, V(1) = 1 (γ = 0.5) time, discounting, episode boundaries
    4 Action always 0 \(1 - (a_i - t_i)^2\), targets −0.6, 0, 0.6 action = \(t_i\) the policy gradient, each agent's own reward
    5 Obs → action ±1, random per agent \(1 - (a_i - o_i/2)^2\) action = \(o_i/2\) the policy uses the agent's own observation
    6 Agent order as 4, agent names agent_2, agent_10, agent_1 as 4 as 4 agents stay matched when names sort differently

    "Observation always 0" means each agent receives the number 0: the network still gets an input, but it carries no information, so the problem becomes "learn one constant". That removes input processing from the list of suspects.

    Measurements.

    • The learned networks are read directly (probe.py): after training, the learner's value and action outputs are queried in the probe's states and compared with the correct answer (tolerance 0.15; values 0.15 times the size of the answer). Return curves are not used to judge a probe; they mix in exploration noise.
    • Which networks training changes: each group of parameters is copied before training and compared after. A network that does not move is not being trained.
    • Learner health, as Jones recommends: entropy, KL between old and new policy, policy and value loss, and whether any loss is NaN or infinite (local.py).

    Controls. DTDE uses RLlib's own network for each agent, so it is the control group: a probe that DTDE passes is solvable with these settings and this budget, and a paradigm that fails it fails because of MARLEM's code. Probes 1-3 passing in a grouped paradigm clears the grouping (rewards, observations, episode ends) before its policy is examined.

    Settings. PPO with the original's settings except the learning rate (3e-4, the usual PPO value; the original starts at 1e-5 and relies on population-based training to raise it), 1000 environment steps per iteration, 10 iterations for probes 1-3 and 20 for 4-6. Training runs in one process with the trainer's own algorithm configuration (build_local), not through Ray Tune; the Tune path is not tested yet.

    Results (probe_paradigms.ipynb):

    Probe DTDE CTCE CTDE
    1 Constant pass pass (3.04 for 3) pass (2.98 for 3)
    2 Observation pass pass, but the loss went NaN (R-02) pass
    3 Discount pass pass pass
    4 Action pass fail: −0.80, 0.01, 0.73 fail: −0.01, 0.19, 0.11
    5 Obs → action pass fail: largest error 0.18 fail: largest error 0.67
    6 Agent order pass pass fail: largest error 0.50

    A second notebook, validate_rl.ipynb, trains the three paradigms on two slightly larger tasks with known best returns and gives the same picture: DTDE learns, CTCE learns slowly, CTDE does not learn.

    What the tests confirm works¶

    Not every check found a problem. These parts behave correctly in every paradigm:

    What Shown by
    Rewards reach the learner; the grouped reward is the sum over agents ([P2] eq. 17) Probe 1: grouped value 3.04 and 2.98 for 3 agents
    Each agent's observation reaches the value network, and the critic tells agents apart Probe 2, including the mixed state (+1, −1, +1 → 1)
    Episodes end where they should; discounting uses the configured γ Probe 3 with γ = 0.5

    1. Training paradigms¶

    I-48: CTDE never trains the policies that act¶

    Fidelity · Tested

    src/environment/ctde.py L46-L57

    # Centralized training: encoder and policy heads for centralized actor, and value head for centralized critic
self.pi_encoder = self._get_encoder_network(concat_obs_dim, embeddings_dim)
self.pi = self._get_head_network(embeddings_dim, concat_act_dim * 2)
self.vf_head = self._get_head_network(embeddings_dim, 1)

# Decentralized execution: each agent gets its own separate policy heads
self.pi_heads  = torch.nn.ModuleDict({
    agent_id: self._get_network(input_dim=tuple_obs_space[i].shape[0],
                                output_dim=tuple_act_space[i].shape[0] * 2,
                                embedding_dim=embeddings_dim)
    for i, agent_id in enumerate(self.agents_id)})

    src/environment/ctde.py L86-L89, L104

    # _forward_inference: used to act, during training and after it
obs_tuple = batch[Columns.OBS]["grouped_agents"]
action_logits = [self.pi_heads[agent_id](obs_tuple[i]) for i, agent_id in enumerate(self.agents_id)]
return {Columns.ACTION_DIST_INPUTS: torch.cat(action_logits, dim=-1)}

# _forward_exploration: used to collect training data
return self._forward_inference(batch, **kwargs)

    src/environment/ctde.py L121-L128

    # _forward_train: what the loss is computed from
concat_obs = torch.cat(batch[Columns.OBS]["grouped_agents"], dim=-1)
central_embeddings = self.pi_encoder(concat_obs)
central_action_logits = self.pi(central_embeddings)

fwd_out = {Columns.ACTION_DIST_INPUTS: central_action_logits,
           Columns.EMBEDDINGS: central_embeddings}

    Why it is wrong. The module holds two actors. The agents act with pi_heads, one network per agent on its own observation, in data collection, evaluation and inference. The loss is computed from pi, a central actor on all observations. Nothing connects the two: pi_heads appear nowhere else except the target-network bookkeeping, so no gradient ever reaches them, and every CTDE run acts with randomly initialised networks, however long it trains.

    [P1] §3.7.1 and [P2] §3.4.2 describe CTDE correctly: a centralised critic during training, and "each agent i retains an independent policy network \(\pi_{\theta_i}(a_i \mid o_i)\)", updated by [P2] eq. 18,

    \[\nabla_{\theta_i} J(\theta_i) = \mathbb{E}\left[\nabla_{\theta_i} \log \pi_{\theta_i}(a_i \mid o_i)\; Q_\phi(s, a_1, \dots, a_I)\right].\]

    The gradient is taken with respect to \(\theta_i\), the parameters of the network that acts. The code instead takes it with respect to the central actor's. The comments show the mix-up: "centralized training" was implemented as "train a central actor", while in CTDE "centralized" refers only to what the critic may see.

    The PPO update of the central actor is itself distorted. PPO's ratio compares the new and old probability of an action under the same policy; here the actions were sampled by the heads, so the ratio compares two different networks, starts far from 1 and is mostly clipped.

    The defect is in _forward_train, which PPO, APPO and SAC all use, so all three CTDE combinations are affected. Inference acts through the same heads.

    Test.

    • Hypothesis. If the acting networks are never trained, CTDE passes the value probes (the critic is trained) and fails every policy probe, its actions do not move toward the answer, and the pi_heads parameters do not change at all.
    • Test. Probes 1-6, plus the change of each parameter group over training.
    • Result. CTDE passes probes 1-3 and fails 4-6. In probe 4 it outputs −0.01, 0.19 and 0.11 for targets −0.6, 0 and 0.6. Over two iterations the parameters change by: pi_encoder 0.150, pi 0.114, vf_head 0.168, pi_heads 0.000. On the toy task (validate_rl.ipynb) CTDE ends at −26.3, worse than always acting 0 (−10.1).
    • What it tells us. The behaviour (policy probes fail, value probes pass), the mechanism (zero change in exactly the acting networks) and the code (heads not on the loss's path) agree. The defect is confirmed and fully explained. Only PPO was run; APPO and SAC follow from the code, not from a test.

    I-60: CTCE is a centralised-information baseline, not an upper bound¶

    Paper · Tested

    src/environment/train.py L676-L687

    def step(self, action_dict: MultiAgentDict) -> Tuple[...]:
    obs, rewards, terminateds, truncateds, infos = self.env.step(action_dict)
    grouped_obs = {k: tuple(v) for k, v in obs.items()}
    # The reward for the group is the sum of individual rewards
    grouped_reward = sum(rewards.values())
    return grouped_obs, grouped_reward, terminateds["__all__"], truncateds["__all__"], infos

    Why it is wrong. [P1] §3.7.1 calls CTCE "a theoretical upper bound for system performance" and [P2] §3.4.1 "the theoretical upper bound for coordination quality", and [P2] reports DTDE as a percentage of it ("91.7 % relative to the theoretical centralized benchmark"). CTCE sees everything, so it can represent the best joint policy, but it is trained by the same approximate learner on a harder problem: one network outputs every agent's action and learns from the sum of all rewards. The learning signal for agent i's action carries every other agent's reward noise, the classic credit-assignment problem that [P1] itself names as CTDE's motivation. With a finite budget, CTCE can end below the decentralised paradigms; "upper bound" holds at best in the limit.

    Test.

    • Hypothesis. With individual rewards, CTCE learns the same policy as DTDE but more slowly, because its gradient for each agent is noisier; it is not broken.
    • Test. Probes 4-6 and the toy tasks, with the same settings and budget as DTDE.
    • Result. On probes 4 and 5 CTCE moves every action in the right direction and order, and its entropy falls steadily (4.06 → 0.07 over 3 dimensions), but after 20 iterations it is still up to 0.20 off (DTDE: 0.06). It passes probe 6. On the toy task it reaches −17.3 where DTDE reaches −3.7; on the team-reward task it is close to DTDE (−2.1 against −1.8). Clipping actions to [−1, 1] explains at most 0.04 of the error at CTCE's final spread (computed).
    • What it tells us. Consistent with "slow, not broken", but not proven: a longer CTCE run on probes 4 and 5 should pass if the hypothesis is right. That run is prepared and has not been run yet. Either way, [P2]'s percentages "of the CTCE benchmark" depend on how long CTCE was trained, not only on the paradigms.

    I-73: returns of the paradigms are compared across different scales¶

    Paper · Judgment

    Same lines as I-60.

    Why it is wrong. CTCE and CTDE optimise the sum of all agents' rewards ([P2] eq. 17); DTDE optimises each agent's own return (eq. 19). [P2] Table 9 ranks the nine combinations by one "mean episode reward". RLlib's reported episode return for a multi-agent run is also the sum over agents, so the numbers may well be on the same scale, but the papers do not say which number Table 9 shows, and a per-agent mean would be smaller by the number of agents. The comparison needs one stated team score, computed from the raw rewards the same way for every paradigm.

    How to settle it. Decide the team score (sum or mean over agents, from raw environment rewards) and report it alongside per-agent returns for every paradigm.

    R-03: the paradigms train different networks¶

    Code · Read

    src/environment/train.py L337-L341

    # DTDE: RLlib's default module for each agent, default model config
agents_rl_module = {agent_id: RLModuleSpec(module_class=self.algorithm.get_module_class(),
                                           observation_space=agents_policy[agent_id].observation_space,
                                           action_space=agents_policy[agent_id].action_space)
                    for agent_id in self.env.agents_id}

    src/environment/ctce.py L290-L306

    # CTCE and CTDE: custom encoder, embeddings_dim = 128 (train.py L166)
return torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(input_dim, embedding_dim, bias=bias),
                           torch.nn.ReLU(),
                           torch.nn.Linear(embedding_dim, embedding_dim, bias=bias),
                           torch.nn.ReLU())

    Why it is wrong. DTDE uses RLlib's default network: two hidden layers of 256, tanh, and a limit on the action spread. CTCE and CTDE use a custom network: two layers of 128, ReLU (CTDE's acting heads add a third), and no limit on the spread (R-01). A difference in results between paradigms is therefore partly a difference in network size, activation and safeguards. [P2] attributes its 3 × 3 differences to the paradigms and algorithms alone.

    How to test it. Give all paradigms the same network and safeguards (or record the difference as part of the paradigm), then repeat the comparison.

    2. Grouping the agents¶

    I-59: grouped spaces are in name order, grouped data in environment order¶

    Code · Read

    src/environment/train.py L646-L648, L702-L705

    # GroupedLEM.__init__: RLlib packs the agents' data in env.agents_id order
self.env = env.with_agent_groups(groups={"grouped_agents": env.agents_id},
                                 obs_space=_tuple_obs_space,
                                 act_space=_tuple_act_space)

# _dict_to_tuple_space: the spaces are put in sorted name order
sorted_keys = sorted(dict_space.keys())
tuple_of_spaces = tuple(dict_space[key] for key in sorted_keys)

    Why it is wrong. The k-th entry of the grouped observation and action is agent agents_id[k], but the k-th grouped space belongs to the k-th name in alphabetical order. They differ whenever the names do not sort in environment order, e.g. agent_1 … agent_10 (alphabetically agent_1, agent_10, agent_2, …).

    Where the spaces have the same shape, data still lands in the right place, but RLlib uses the action bounds: by default (normalize_actions=True) it rescales each policy output from [−1, 1] to its space's [low, high]. In the market, each agent's quantity bound is its own capacity (action.py L35-L44). With such names, in CTCE and CTDE an agent's bid would be scaled to another agent's capacity.

    Test so far. Probe 6 (agents named agent_2, agent_10, agent_1) passes for DTDE and CTCE: observations, actions and rewards stay matched. But all probe agents have identical bounds, so the probe cannot show the wrong scaling.

    How to test it. Probe 6 with different action bounds per agent: the correct action of each agent is known in its own units, so a scaling with another agent's bounds shows as a wrong answer.

    3. Policy networks and loss¶

    R-01: the CTCE and CTDE action spread has no limit¶

    Code · Tested

    src/environment/ctce.py L45, L309-L322, L365

    self.pi = self._get_head_network(embeddings_dim, concat_act_dim * 2)  # mean + log_std

# _get_head_network
return torch.nn.Linear(embedding_dim, output_dim, bias=bias)

# _get_distribution_class
child_dist_cls = TorchDiagGaussian

    src/environment/ctde.py L360-L364 (the per-agent heads end the same way)

    Why it is wrong. For each action the policy outputs a mean and a log standard deviation \(\log\sigma\), and the action is drawn from a Gaussian. Its entropy per dimension is \(1.42 + \log\sigma\), and PPO's loss rewards entropy (coefficient 0.01). Where the reward does not depend on the action, the reward gradient averages to zero and the entropy bonus is the only steady force on \(\log\sigma\), so it grows for as long as training runs. RLlib's own networks clip \(\log\sigma\) to ±20 (its PPO default, log_std_clip_param = 20). MARLEM's custom heads are a plain linear layer feeding TorchDiagGaussian, which does not clip, so \(\log\sigma\) can grow until \(\sigma^2\) overflows the float32 range. In the market, a step where an agent's bid cannot change its reward (no counterparty, a full battery) creates exactly this condition.

    Test.

    • Hypothesis. In probe 2 (reward independent of the action) DTDE's entropy stops where RLlib's clip sits, while CTCE's and CTDE's grows without limit until it overflows.
    • Test. Probe 2, with the learner's entropy and KL logged every iteration.
    • Result. DTDE's entropy ends at 21.42, exactly \(1.42 + 20\), RLlib's clip. CTCE reaches 147.9 and CTDE 151.3 over 3 dimensions, about \(\log\sigma \approx 48\) per dimension; \(\sigma^2\) exceeds float32, and the KL between old and new policy becomes infinite. At the original learning rate (1e-5) CTCE did not diverge within 6 iterations; at 3e-4 it diverged within 4.
    • What it tells us. The missing clip is confirmed, and RLlib's clip is the exact difference. The speed depends on the learning rate and the entropy coefficient, both of which the original's population-based training raises (I-49). In CTDE the network that diverged is the central actor, which never acts (I-48); once the heads are trained, they have the same unbounded output.

    R-02: kl_coeff = 0 does not switch off the KL term, and a NaN loss goes unnoticed¶

    Code · Tested

    src/environment/train.py L216-L226

    return (PPOConfig()
        .training(lr=1e-5,
                  # ...
                  num_epochs=30,
                  minibatch_size=128,
                  kl_coeff=0.0,
                  entropy_coeff=0.01,
                  clip_param=0.2,
                  grad_clip=0.5))

    Why it is wrong. Setting kl_coeff=0.0 looks like it removes PPO's KL penalty. In RLlib it does not: use_kl_loss stays on by default, so the learner still computes the KL between the old and new policy and adds \(0 \cdot \text{KL}\) to the loss. When the KL is infinite (R-01), \(0 \cdot \infty\) is NaN in floating point, and the whole loss is NaN. RLlib's default response (torch_skip_nan_gradients = False) is to replace NaN gradients with zero, log a warning and step on, warning itself that this "can corrupt the internal state of the optimizer". Training continues, metrics keep arriving, and the policy has stopped learning. The value network does not depend on the KL and keeps learning, so value metrics look healthy.

    Test.

    • Hypothesis. In the iteration where the KL becomes infinite, the total loss is NaN while the policy loss is still finite (the NaN comes from \(0 \cdot \infty\), not from the policy itself), and the value network keeps learning afterwards.
    • Test. Probe 2 with CTCE, logging total loss, policy loss and KL each iteration.
    • Result. At iteration 4 (learning rate 3e-4): KL = ∞, total loss NaN, policy loss 0.005. From the next iteration on the policy loss is NaN too. The value check of probe 2 still passes (error 0.001). RLlib's log fills with "Gradients … contain nan/inf values" warnings (about 200 MB of them in the notebook).
    • What it tells us. The mechanism is confirmed: a divergence that should stop a run instead degrades it silently. Any run of the original should be checked for NaN losses before its results are read.

    R-04: SAC in CTCE and CTDE uses an unsquashed Gaussian¶

    Code · Read

    src/environment/ctce.py L358-L373

    def _get_distribution_class(self) -> TorchMultiDistribution:
    tuple_space = self.action_space["grouped_agents"]
    child_dist_cls = TorchDiagGaussian
    # ... one TorchDiagGaussian per agent, for every algorithm

    Why it is wrong. RLlib's SAC uses a tanh-squashed Gaussian for bounded actions (TorchSquashedGaussian), so that sampled actions stay in bounds and the log-probabilities and entropy in SAC's loss ([P2] eq. 15) are those of the actions actually taken. The custom modules give SAC the same plain Gaussian as PPO, for both CTCE and CTDE, while SAC-DTDE uses RLlib's squashed one. So SAC differs between paradigms in its action distribution, and in CTCE and CTDE the entropy target (\(-\sum \dim A_i\), train.py L240) applies to an unbounded distribution whose samples are clipped afterwards. [P2] attributes SAC's very different results across paradigms ("excellent in CTCE and CTDE … failing in DTDE") to the paradigms.

    How to test it. Probes 1-6 with SAC in each paradigm. SAC has no value function, so its value probes check the Q-values instead.

    R-05: SAC's actor in CTCE and CTDE gets no gradient from the critic¶

    Code · Read

    src/environment/ctce.py L132-L146 (ctde.py L143-L157 is the same)

    # SECOND PASS: Compute Q-values for actions sampled from current policy to train the actor
curr_action_dist = self.action_dist_cls.from_logits(action_logits)
curr_actions_resampled = curr_action_dist.sample()
# ...
fwd_out["q_curr"] = torch.min(self.qf(curr_qf_embeddings).squeeze(-1),
                              self.qf_twin(curr_qf_twin_embeddings).squeeze(-1))
fwd_out["logp_resampled"] = curr_action_dist.logp(curr_actions_resampled)

    Why it is wrong. SAC trains its actor on \(L_\pi = \alpha \log\pi_\theta(\tilde a \mid s) - \min_j Q_j(s, \tilde a)\) ([P2] eq. 15). The second term is how the actor learns from the critic: \(\tilde a\) must be a reparameterised sample, \(\tilde a = m_\theta(s) + \sigma_\theta(s)\,\varepsilon\), so that the gradient flows through \(\partial Q / \partial \tilde a\) into \(\theta\). RLlib's own SAC module does this with rsample() (default_sac_torch_rl_module.py L155). The custom modules call sample(), which draws without a gradient (checked: sample() returns a tensor with requires_grad=False, rsample() one with True). The \(Q\) term is then a constant for the actor, and \(\partial L_\pi / \partial\theta\) comes from \(\alpha \log\pi_\theta\) alone: the actor is trained only to raise its entropy, never toward higher Q-values. The critic still learns, so Q metrics look healthy.

    So SAC-DTDE (RLlib's module) is SAC, but SAC-CTCE and SAC-CTDE are not: their policies drift toward maximum spread regardless of the reward. In CTDE this adds to I-48 (the trained actor does not even act). [P2] reports SAC as "excellent in CTCE and CTDE … failing in DTDE".

    How to test it. Probe 4 with SAC-CTCE: the action means should stay where they start while the entropy rises; with rsample() they should move to the targets. Directly: one SAC loss on a batch, then check that the actor's parameters receive a gradient from the \(Q\) term (zero now).

    I-49: population-based training jumps the learning rate and reaches entropy 10¶

    Code · Read

    src/environment/train.py L178-L189

    Code · Read

    Problems in the papers' RL method¶

    What the papers leave wrong or open, whatever the code does. Each needs a recorded decision.

    Topic The problem in [P1] / [P2] Issues
    CTCE as upper bound Called a theoretical upper bound, but trained by the same approximate learner on the summed reward; it learns more slowly I-60
    Comparing returns One "mean episode reward" across paradigms whose objectives are a group sum (eq. 17) and per-agent returns (eq. 19); the score is not defined I-73
    CTDE critic Eq. 18 uses a joint-action critic \(Q_\phi(s, \mathbf{a})\); PPO and APPO learn a state-value critic \(V(s)\). "CTDE" names a family, not one method I-48
    Budget "10 000 episodes"; the code counts iterations I-50
    Evaluation One seed, evaluation on the training scenario, ranking by the training reward I-72
    Network design Not described; the paradigms use different networks (R-03) and the custom ones lack RLlib's safeguards (R-01, R-04) R-03

    What this means for the published results¶

    [P1] is not affected. Its results use zero-intelligence agents (random bids); it states that "full MARL training is deferred to future work". The defects above affect the framework [P1] presents, including its highlight of "the first true, like-for-like comparison of … CTCE, DTDE and CTDE", not its numbers.

    [P2] trains the 3 × 3 matrix. Its hyperparameters (Table 8: learning rate 1e-5, entropy 0.01, clip 0.2, gradient clip 0.5, minibatch 128, buffer 1000) are the original code's. If its runs used this code, which cannot be confirmed independently:

    • Every CTDE result comes from untrained policies (I-48). PPO-CTDE (mean 3004.8), APPO-CTDE and SAC-CTDE (mean 3158.2) acted with the heads' initial weights. [P2] reads SAC-CTDE's low standard deviation (42.5) as "the centralized critic successfully guided the local actors"; a policy that never changes also varies little. That untrained policies score close to CTCE (3099-3144) also says the reward hardly separates trained from untrained behaviour, a question for the connection document.
    • CTCE and CTDE trials may have run with NaN losses (R-01, R-02, I-49). With population-based training raising the entropy coefficient toward 10, trials of the grouped paradigms are exactly the ones whose spread can diverge, and the original would keep reporting them.
    • SAC-CTCE and SAC-CTDE policies were never steered by their critics (R-05). Their actors only gained entropy, so [P2]'s SAC results in the grouped paradigms ("excellent in CTCE and CTDE") come from policies that did not learn from the reward.
    • APPO's λ search found nothing (R-06): lambda_ has no effect in APPO.
    • The ranking mixes paradigm with network (R-03, R-04) and with training length (I-60), on one seed (I-72).

    So [P2]'s conclusions about CTDE ("a robust middle ground", "the optimal choice" for SAC) are not supported by the original code, and its comparisons between paradigms need the fixes above before they can be repeated.
