    Market layer: what was wrong and what changed¶

    Every problem found in the market layer of MARLEM: in the original code, in the paper [P1], or in the method itself. For each one, what was wrong, where it came from, and what marlem-v1 does about it. The reinforcement-learning layer (actions, observations, reward, KPIs, training) is validated separately, starting with the training layer in rl.md, and is left out here.

    Scope and sources¶

    Market layer means everything that turns orders into trades, money and energy:

    • order validation and the three clearing stages;
    • pricing mechanisms;
    • the DSO (stage 3, balance, fees);
    • transmission losses;
    • settlement, the battery and each participant's balance and profit;
    • reputation and the trade ledger;
    • the grid model as the market uses it.

    Sources.

    • IDs are those of the issue register, the single list of every issue in the original; this page gives the detail for the market layer.
    • The original code is the authors' MARLEM repository, salazarna/marlem, at its current main (commit 15d6715, March 2026). Every code link and snippet points there, pinned to that commit, so the line numbers stay valid.
    • IDs N-xx are findings made while rebuilding the market.
    • [P1] is the MARLEM paper (Salazar-Peña et al., Applied Energy 412, 2026); its method is summarised in methodology.md.

    Origin (as in the register):

    The most consequential ones for any result produced with the original:

    • Batteries ran backwards. Selling energy charged the battery (I-18), and the discharge efficiency was applied the wrong way round (I-20).
    • Two pricing mechanisms returned the buyer's bid. One was SELLER (I-13). The other was BID_ASK_SPREAD (I-17), which the paper's own case study uses.
    • The money and the DSO balance never moved. Profit was never updated (I-27), fees were never charged (I-28), and the DSO balance stayed at 0 (I-24).
    • Reputation only ever measured one thing. Reliability was always 1 and the grid part was always neutral, so only price fairness moved. The decay was weighted by the time of day (I-38).
    • The grid's edge flows started random and piled up over the day (I-33). Only stage-1 trades fed them (I-32).

    How to read an issue¶

    Each issue below has the same parts:

    • Header: ID, title, origin and status in marlem-v1.
    • Original code: the lines at fault, copied from the original repository (salazarna/marlem, commit 15d6715). The caption links to them on GitHub with their line numbers. # ... stands for lines cut for length, and a few long calls are shortened with ....
    • Why it is wrong: what the lines do, which other code calls them or reads their result, and how that differs from [P1]. Where a definition looks harmless on its own, this part shows the call site that makes it wrong.
    • In marlem-v1: what the rebuild does, with a link, and the new code when it is short enough to compare.

    Fixed green: fixed, and you're fine with what was done Review yellow: the AI fixed it, but you want to look again Open red: still open

    1. The market step¶

    I-01: only 23 of 24 periods run¶

    Fidelity · Fixed

    src/environment/lem.py L433-L445

    # STEP 4. Update environment state
self.current_step += 1
# ...
# STEP 5. Check if episode is done
if self.current_step >= self.max_steps - 1:
    terminateds = {agent_id: True for agent_id in self.agents_id}

    Why it is wrong. The counter is raised before the check, and the check is against \(\text{max_steps} - 1\). With \(\text{max_steps} = 24\), the step that played period 22 raises the counter to 23, and \(23 \ge 23\) ends the episode. Periods 0-22 run, and period 23 (the last hour of the day) never does (T1). Every episode loses its last hour of demand and generation.

    In marlem-v1. A day is \(24 / \text{step_hours}\) steps, and done is true only after all of them are recorded (lem.py).

    I-78: an invalid order stops the simulation¶

    Fidelity · Fixed

    Review This raises a bigger topic, how to deal with invalid actions across all the features

    src/environment/lem.py L336-L343

    for id in self.agents_id:
    action = action_dict[id]
    action_dict[id] = self._agents[id].adjust_action_for_battery(...)

    if not self.action_handler.is_valid_action(id, action_dict[id]):

    Why it is wrong. [P1] Algorithm 1 keeps an order only if ValidateOrder(o_i, S_t): an invalid order is dropped, and the market goes on. Here one bad action raises an exception, which ends the whole episode.

    I-56: units and time step are mixed¶

    Paper · Fixed

    src/market/matching.py L33-L36, src/agent/der.py L35, src/agent/battery.py L99, L117, L135, L162

    # MarketConfig
min_price: float = 0.0  # Minimum allowed price ($/Wh)
max_price: float = 1000.0  #  Maximum allowed price($/Wh)
min_quantity: float = 0.0  # Minimum allowed quantity (Wh)
max_quantity: float = 1000.0  # Maximum allowed quantity (Wh)

# DERAgent docstring
capacity: Maximum capacity of the DER (W)

# Battery docstrings
energy: Charging energy in kWh        # _can_charge
energy: Discharge energy in kWh       # _can_discharge
energy: Charging energy in Wh         # charge
energy: Discharging energy in Wh      # discharge

    Why it is wrong. The market's own configuration says prices are in $/Wh and quantities in Wh. The battery's methods say kWh for the checks and Wh for the actual charging, and the DER capacity is in W. [P1]'s case study prices in $/MWh (35-280), and [P2] plots prices in "$/kWh". There is no step length anywhere, so "quantity" means energy per 1-hour step only by convention. Between the comments, the code and the two papers, a price differs by up to a factor of a million ($/Wh vs $/MWh), and any number that mixes energy, power and price is ambiguous.

    In marlem-v1. Energy is kWh per step, power is kW, prices are per kWh, and the step length is explicit: GridSetup.step_hours, 1 h or 15 min (scenario.py). Power becomes energy with \(\text{kW} \cdot \text{step_hours}\) (der.py).

    I-05, I-06: a reset cannot be told which profiles to use¶

    Code / Fidelity · Replaced

    src/environment/lem.py L281-L293

    for agent in self._agents.values():
    reputation = self.reputation_handler.reset(seed)
    generation, demand = self.der_profile_handler.reset(agent.capacity,
                                                        self.max_steps)
    agent.reset(reputation, generation, demand, seed)

    src/profile/base.py L40-L43, src/profile/der.py L119-L129

    df = pd.read_csv(path)
selected_col = np.random.choice(df.columns)       # a random column
return df[selected_col].tolist()

base_profile = self._load_profile(self.generation_file_path)
scale_factor = np.random.uniform(1.0, 1.3)        # and a random scale
# ...
    varied_value = value * scale_factor * (1 + noise)

    src/profile/der.py L187-L198

    base_value = effective_max_value * np.exp(-normalized_hour**2)
cloud_noise = np.random.uniform(-0.2 * cloudiness, 0.2 * cloudiness)
noise = np.random.uniform(-noise_magnitude, noise_magnitude)
value = max(0.0, (base_value + noise + cloud_noise) * capacity)

    Why it is wrong. The reset has no way to say which profiles an episode should use, so the market cannot be put back into a defined starting state at all:

    • Profiles given to an agent are thrown away (I-05). DERAgent(...) takes generation and demand profiles, but reset() always replaces them with the profile handler's (T11). reset() itself takes no day or profile argument. There is no supported way to run the market on a chosen day, or to run the same day again.
    • What the handler produces is not a choice of day (I-06). A CSV profile is a random column of the file at a random 1.0-1.3 scale. Depending on what the columns hold, that is either a different household for the same agent, or a different day with an invented scale; the code does not say which.
    • With a seed, nothing varies; without one, nothing is reproducible. The reset reseeds the global random generator with the configured seed whenever no seed is passed (I-03). With a seed set, as in [P2] (seed 42), every episode draws the same profiles. Without one, no two runs can be compared. Neither is a defined rule.
    • The procedural profile is not eq. 17. [P1] eq. 17 is multiplicative, \(G = C \cdot I \cdot (1 + \varepsilon_{\text{noise}} + \varepsilon_{\text{cloud}})\). The code adds the noise to the irradiance shape instead, \((I + \varepsilon_{\text{noise}} + \varepsilon_{\text{cloud}}) \cdot C\), so the noise does not scale with the sun and appears at full size at dawn and dusk.

    The market needs a reset with a clear rule: the same scenario and day give the same profiles, and an agent stays the same agent (same elements, same load type) whatever day is played. The original offers no such reset.

    Outside the market layer. Whether profiles should differ between episodes is a reinforcement-learning question, not a market one. For training, playing a different day each episode (e.g. sampled from a year of data, with held-out days for evaluation) is desirable, so that agents generalise instead of memorising one day. That needs exactly the controllable reset the original lacks: the RL layer chooses the day, the market builds it. Under [P2]'s fixed seed, the original instead trains on the same profiles in every episode.

    In marlem-v1. Generation and demand come from the SimBench profiles of the network elements a participant owns, for the scenario's day (GridSetup.day), and reset() rebuilds exactly that day every time (der.py). Eq. 17 is not used. Choosing a different day per episode is not built yet: the grid contract already provides for it (GridModel.reset(episode_seed, day), contract.py), but LocalEnergyMarket.reset() does not take a day yet. That is for the RL layer to call.

    2. Orders and feasibility¶

    I-08: feasibility clipping is not eq. 15¶

    Fidelity · Fixed

    src/agent/der.py L184-L228

    price, quantity, is_buy, preferred_partner = action
# ...
if is_buy:
    # Calculate how much energy we need to buy after using our own generation
    energy_deficit = quantity - current_generation
    # ...
    quantity = max(0.0, energy_deficit)
else:
    # Calculate how much energy we can export after using our own generation
    energy_surplus = quantity - current_demand
    # ...
    quantity = max(0.0, energy_surplus)

    Why it is wrong. [P1] eq. 15 bounds an order by the agent's physical position:

    • \(q_{\text{sell,max}} = \max(0, G - D) + E_{\text{dischargeable}}\)
    • \(q_{\text{buy,max}} = \max(0, D - G) + E_{\text{chargeable}}\)

    The code instead treats the requested quantity as if it were the agent's demand (buying) or generation (selling) and subtracts the other side from it. A buyer with \(G = 0,\ D = 5\) that asks for 2 kWh is not limited to 5 + its battery room: its "deficit" is \(2 - 0\), and its battery may then cover that, so the order shrinks to 0. A seller with \(G = 10,\ D = 1\) asking to sell 3 kWh gets \(3 - 1 = 2\). The limit has nothing to do with what the agent can actually deliver or take.

    In marlem-v1. Eq. 15a-b, with the battery's room from eq. 15c-d (der.py):

    def max_sell_kwh(self, step: int) -> float:
    """Eq. 15a: q_sell,max = max(0, G_t − D_t) + E_dischargeable."""
    return max(0.0, self.net_kwh(step)) + self.battery_room()[1]

    I-09: an order can be sized as a buy and submitted as a sell¶

    Fidelity · Fixed by design

    src/agent/der.py L196, src/environment/lem.py L358

    # DERAgent.adjust_action_for_battery: sizing the order
if is_buy:
    energy_deficit = quantity - current_generation
    # ...

# LocalEnergyMarket.step: submitting it
is_buy = bool(is_buy > 0.5)

    Why it is wrong. The action's direction is a continuous number. The sizing code reads it as truthy (any non-zero value is a buy); the submitting code reads it as > 0.5. A raw value of 0.3 is sized with the buy logic, then submitted as a sell order (T8): the quantity was computed for the wrong side of the market.

    In marlem-v1. The side is a boolean in OrderIntent, set once. Turning a continuous action into a side is RL-layer work.

    I-11: no way to submit nothing¶

    Paper · Fixed at the market layer

    src/agent/der.py L231-L232

    price = max(market_config.min_price, min(market_config.max_price, price))
quantity = max(market_config.min_quantity, min(market_config.max_quantity, quantity))

    Why it is wrong. After clipping for the battery the quantity can be 0, but it is then raised to min_quantity. With \(q_{\min} > 0\), an agent that has nothing to trade still submits an order of \(q_{\min}\). [P1] eq. 2 has no "no order" action either.

    In marlem-v1. A participant with nothing to trade places no order, and _validate drops orders at or below \(q_{\min}\). Whether an RL action can express "nothing" is left to the RL layer.

    I-12: clearing overwrites the submitted orders¶

    Code · Fixed

    src/market/matching.py L347-L348, src/market/dso.py L232, L253

    # Stage 2
buy_order.quantity -= (proposed_quantity - transmission_loss)
sell_order.quantity -= proposed_quantity

# Stage 3 (DSO), for every remaining order
sell_order.quantity = 0
buy_order.quantity = 0

    src/environment/lem.py L424-L425, src/environment/reward.py L406

    rewards = {id: self.reward_handler.calculate_reward(agent, orders[id], ...)
# ...
execution_ratio = executed_quantity / order.quantity if order.quantity > 0 else 0

    Why it is wrong. The clearing stages subtract matched quantities from the order objects themselves, and the DSO sets every remaining one to 0. After clearing, every order in orders has quantity 0. Everything that reads the orders afterwards (the reward, the KPIs, the info dictionary) sees 0 instead of what was submitted (T3). The reward's order.quantity > 0 guard then makes every execution ratio 0. This is the root cause of the RL layer's I-43.

    In marlem-v1. Every stage works on copies, work = [replace(o) for o in orders], and returns the remaining orders separately; the submitted orders are never changed (clearing.py).

    3. Clearing stages¶

    I-15: DSO-preferred orders are cleared before the peer market¶

    Paper · Decided

    src/market/matching.py L608-L623

    # STAGE 1: Handle DSO preferences first
dso_preferred_orders = [o for o in orders if o.partner_id == self.dso.id]
# ...
if dso_preferred_orders:
    dso_trades, _ = self.dso.clear_unmatched_orders(current_step,
                                                    dso_preferred_buys,
                                                    dso_preferred_sells)
    preference_trades.extend(dso_trades)

    Why it is wrong. [P1] §3.3.2 has three stages: mutual preferences, the double auction, then the DSO for the residual. The code adds a sub-stage the paper does not have: an order naming the DSO is cleared with the DSO first, before any peer matching. The paper gives no reason for naming the DSO at all, since its prices are the worst in the market.

    In marlem-v1. Orders that prefer the DSO skip stages 1 and 2 and are cleared in stage 3 with the rest (clearing.py, L144).

    I-77: partner choice is a sort and a random pick, not a weighted score¶

    Fidelity · Fixed

    Review How to weight the formula, 50/50 ok?

    src/agent/der.py L154-L159, src/environment/lem.py L362-L365

    candidates = [agent for agent in candidates if agent.id != self.id]
candidates.sort(key=lambda x: (-x.reputation, self.location.distance_to(x.location, graph)))
return np.random.choice(candidates[:max(1, len(candidates) // 2 + 1)]).id if randomize else candidates[0].id

# called from step() with:
preferred_partner = self._agents[id].select_preferred_partner([...], graph, randomize=True)

    Why it is wrong. [P1] §3.4: the rule-based strategy "prioritizes partners based on a weighted score of high reputation and short grid distance". The code does three other things:

    • it sorts by reputation first and uses distance only to break exact ties;
    • it picks at random from the top half;
    • its candidates include agents on the same side of the market, so a seller can name another seller, a preference stage 1 can never match.

    In marlem-v1. A weighted score of reputation and closeness, over participants on the other side of the market only. Both are rescaled over the candidates, 1 for the best and 0 for the worst, so the weight \(w\) (partner_reputation_weight, 0.5) gives each the same say:

    \[ \text{score}_j = w\, \frac{r_j - r_{\min}}{r_{\max} - r_{\min}} + (1 - w)\, \frac{d_{\max} - d_j}{d_{\max} - d_{\min}} \]

    Without the rescaling, reputations that all start at 0.5 and drift by a few hundredths would lose to distance every time. Ties go to the nearer candidate (der.py, lem.py).

    I-55: the stage-2 capacity check can never trigger¶

    Code · Decided

    src/market/matching.py L158, L299-L307

    self.grid_capacity: float = config.max_quantity * self.num_agents
# ...
if abs(grid_balance) > self.grid_capacity:
    # Grid is already at capacity, skip this match
    if grid_balance > 0:
        sell_idx += 1
    else:
        buy_idx += 1
    continue

    Why it is wrong. "Grid capacity" here is \(\text{max_quantity} \cdot N\) (1000 kWh per agent by default), not anything about the grid. It is compared with the current balance, not with the balance after the trade, and that balance is the DSO balance, which stays 0 (I-24). The check never fires, so stage 2 has no grid constraint at all.

    In marlem-v1. The check is gone. [P1] says violating trades "can be penalized or disallowed by the DSO" without choosing. Decided: penalized. The congestion and thermal fees (eq. 8a, 8d) price the loading of the lines on a trade's path in the previous power flow, trades with the DSO included (I-54). The power flow reports voltage and loading violations but blocks no trade. Disallowing would need a grid check per trade, for example with sensitivity factors as in Guerrero, Chapman and Verbič (2019), and a rollback: future work. The episode runs show whether violations occur at all.

    4. Pricing mechanisms¶

    I-13: SELLER pricing returns the buyer's price¶

    What this means for the published results¶

    [P1]'s Case 5 (batteries with zero-intelligence agents) ran on this market. If the paper's figures were produced with the original code, which cannot be confirmed independently:

    • The P2P prices in Fig. 5 are the buyers' bids. The case uses BID_ASK_SPREAD, which returned the buyer's price (I-17).
    • The batteries did not behave as described. Selling charged them (I-18), discharge drained less than it delivered (I-20), and a charge reaching \(SoC_{\max}\) was refused entirely (I-19).
    • No money moved: no profit (I-27), no fees (I-28), no DSO balance (I-24).

    So the paper's conclusion that batteries stabilise prices is not supported by the original market. A thesis built on marlem-v1 is a corrected reimplementation of the paper's market, not a reproduction of its results.
