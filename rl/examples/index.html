    Worked examples: the parts we define¶

    The training cycle with numbers, limited to what our own code decides: how the agents are grouped, how CTDE's per-agent actor is put together, the settings we choose, and how a trained policy is evaluated. Everything else (the networks' layers, sampling, GAE, the losses, Adam) is RLlib's and is described in One training iteration; it appears here only where our code feeds it or reads from it. Step labels (A4, C2, ...) are those of that page.

    The code is the fixed implementation in core/rl/ in this repository. The links point to the local files. Every number below was computed with that code or with RLlib's own functions (unsquash_action), not by hand.

    File What it defines Section here
    grouping.py GroupedEnv: all agents as one, for CTCE and CTDE 1, 2, 4
    ctde_modules.py CTDE: RLlib's modules with one actor per agent 3, 5
    config.py the settings of each of the nine combinations 6
    policy.py acting with a trained policy, baselines, the team score 7
    train.py the budget, the NaN stop, when evaluation runs 8

    The example¶

    Three agents, listed by the environment in the order agent_2, agent_10, agent_1 (alphabetically agent_1, agent_10, agent_2, the case that broke the original, I-59). Each observes 2 numbers and sets 2 actions: a quantity (kWh, negative = sell) and a price (€/kWh). The quantity bound is each agent's own capacity.

    Agent Observation \(o_i\) Quantity bounds Price bounds
    agent_2 (0.4, 0.8) [−5, 5] [0.05, 0.35]
    agent_10 (−0.2, 0.5) [−20, 20] [0.05, 0.35]
    agent_1 (0.9, 0.1) [−2, 2] [0.05, 0.35]

    So \(N = 3\), \(d_o = 2\), \(d_a = 2\).

    1. Grouping the observations (A1, CTCE and CTDE)¶

    GroupedEnv turns the three agents into one agent, "group". Its observation is the agents' observations side by side, in the environment's order (join, grouping.py L55-L59):

    \[s = (\underbrace{0.4,\ 0.8}_{\texttt{agent\_2}},\ \underbrace{-0.2,\ 0.5}_{\texttt{agent\_10}},\ \underbrace{0.9,\ 0.1}_{\texttt{agent\_1}}) \in \mathbb{R}^6.\]

    The action space is joined the same way (join_spaces, L28-L34), each agent's bounds in its own slice:

    Position 1 2 3 4 5 6
    Belongs to agent_2 q agent_2 p agent_10 q agent_10 p agent_1 q agent_1 p
    low −5 0.05 −20 0.05 −2 0.05
    high 5 0.35 20 0.35 2 0.35

    To RLlib the group is an ordinary agent with a 6-dimensional Box observation and action. That is the whole point: RLlib's own module and algorithm then train CTCE without any custom network. In DTDE nothing is grouped: each agent's module gets its own \(o_i\) and its own bounds.

    2. From the policy's output to the market (A3-A5)¶

    What RLlib does (A3). Say the policy outputs, for the 6 dimensions, means and log-stds

    \[m = (0.30,\ -0.10,\ -0.60,\ 0.20,\ 0.10,\ 0.50), \qquad \ell = (-0.50,\ -1.00,\ -0.70,\ -1.20,\ -0.40,\ -0.90),\]

    so \(\sigma = e^\ell = (0.607, 0.368, 0.497, 0.301, 0.670, 0.407)\), and draws the noise \(\varepsilon = (1.0, -0.5, 0.2, 1.5, -2.0, 0.3)\):

    \[u = m + \sigma\varepsilon = (0.907,\ -0.284,\ -0.501,\ 0.652,\ -1.241,\ 0.622).\]

    What our bounds decide (A4). RLlib rescales each dimension from \([-1, 1]\) to the joint space's bounds and clips, \(a_k = \text{clip}(\text{low}_k + (u_k + 1)(\text{high}_k - \text{low}_k)/2)\):

    Dimension \(u_k\) low, high \(a_k\)
    agent_2 q 0.907 −5, 5 −5 + 1.907 × 5 = 4.53
    agent_2 p −0.284 0.05, 0.35 0.05 + 0.716 × 0.15 = 0.157
    agent_10 q −0.501 −20, 20 −20 + 0.499 × 20 = −10.01
    agent_10 p 0.652 0.05, 0.35 0.298
    agent_1 q −1.241 −2, 2 −2 + (−0.241) × 2 = −2.48 → clipped to −2
    agent_1 p 0.622 0.05, 0.35 0.293

    The same \(u = -0.501\) would be −2.5 kWh for agent_2 and −1.0 kWh for agent_1: the bounds must belong to the right agent. The original put the bounds in sorted name order (agent_1, agent_10, agent_2) but the data in this order, so positions 1-2 carried agent_1's bounds and positions 5-6 agent_2's: agent_2 would have bid \(-2 + 1.907 \times 2 = 1.81\) kWh instead of 4.53, and agent_1 would have been clipped at −5 instead of −2, beyond its own capacity (I-59). agent_10 happens to sit in the middle of both orders and would have been right.

    agent_1's quantity shows the clip: \(u = -1.241\) is outside \([-1, 1]\), so the market gets the bound −2, but the batch keeps \(u = -1.241\) and the learner trains on it.

    Splitting back (A5). GroupedEnv.step cuts the joint action into the agents' parts (split, L46-L52) and passes them to the environment:

    {"agent_2": [4.53, 0.157], "agent_10": [-10.01, 0.298], "agent_1": [-2.0, 0.293]}

    3. CTDE: one actor per agent (A2)¶

    In CTDE each agent's action may depend only on its own observation. Our module (ctde_modules.py L134-L140) is RLlib's PPO module for the group with the actor replaced by three separate ones, each the network RLlib would build for that agent alone (agent_actors, L99-L119). The critic is left as RLlib builds it, on all of \(s\).

    Encoders (AgentEncoders, L48-L66). Agent \(i\)'s encoder reads only its slice of \(s\): agent_2's positions 1-2 = (0.4, 0.8), agent_10's 3-4 = (−0.2, 0.5), agent_1's 5-6 = (0.9, 0.1). Each turns its 2 numbers into a 256-number latent, \(h_i\); the three latents are put side by side (768 numbers).

    Heads (AgentHeads, L69-L96). Head \(i\) reads only \(h_i\) and outputs \([m_{i}, \ell_{i}]\) for its 2 action dimensions. Say:

    Agent Head output \([m_q, m_p, \ell_q, \ell_p]\)
    agent_2 (0.30, −0.10, −0.50, −1.00)
    agent_10 (−0.60, 0.20, −0.70, −1.20)
    agent_1 (0.10, 0.50, −0.40, −0.90)

    RLlib's Gaussian for a 6-dimensional action reads a 12-number input as first the 6 means, then the 6 log-stds (loc, log_std = logits.chunk(2)). So the heads' outputs are rearranged before they leave the module:

    \[(\underbrace{0.30, -0.10}_{m,\ \texttt{agent\_2}}, \underbrace{-0.60, 0.20}_{m,\ \texttt{agent\_10}}, \underbrace{0.10, 0.50}_{m,\ \texttt{agent\_1}}, \underbrace{-0.50, -1.00}_{\ell,\ \texttt{agent\_2}}, \underbrace{-0.70, -1.20}_{\ell,\ \texttt{agent\_10}}, \underbrace{-0.40, -0.90}_{\ell,\ \texttt{agent\_1}}).\]

    These are exactly the \(m\) and \(\ell\) of section 2, so from here on CTDE is the same as CTCE: RLlib samples, rescales, stores and trains a 6-dimensional Gaussian. Had the outputs simply been concatenated, \((0.30, -0.10, -0.50, -1.00, -0.60, 0.20, \dots)\), RLlib would have read agent_2's log-stds as agent_10's means.

    Sizes (RLlib's default network, 2 × 256 tanh, actor and critic separate):

    Actor parameters Critic parameters
    DTDE, per agent 67 588 (input 2, output 4) 66 817 (input 2)
    CTCE 70 668 (input 6, output 12) 67 841 (input 6)
    CTDE 3 × 67 588 = 202 764 (three DTDE actors) 67 841 (CTCE's critic)

    CTDE's actor is the three DTDE actors, and its critic is CTCE's.

    4. The group's reward (A5, B4)¶

    GroupedEnv.step returns one reward for the group, the sum of the agents' (L104-L123). With the market paying

    \[r_{\texttt{agent\_2}} = 1.50, \qquad r_{\texttt{agent\_10}} = -0.50, \qquad r_{\texttt{agent\_1}} = 0.25,\]

    the group gets \(R = 1.25\). The agents' own rewards travel along in the group's infos (for evaluation), but the learner sees only 1.25.

    What follows from it, all computed by RLlib:

    • The critic \(V(s)\) learns the group's return. Its targets are about \(N = 3\) times the size of one agent's, which matters for RLlib's value-loss cap of 10 (cycle.md C4).
    • One advantage for the group. \(\hat A\) is computed from \(R\), so agent_1's bid is reinforced or discouraged by the same number as agent_2's, although here agent_10 lost money and agent_2 earned it. Telling the agents' contributions apart is left to the averaging over many steps (the credit-assignment cost, I-60).

    In DTDE each agent's module learns from its own \(r_i\) and nothing is summed.

    5. CTDE in the update: what the per-agent actor means for the loss (C2, C3, C8)¶

    The loss is RLlib's; what our module decides is that the joint policy is a product of the agents' policies. Here is what that does in numbers.

    Log-probability. For the stored \(u\) of section 2, per dimension \(\log\pi = -\frac{(u - m)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \ell - \frac12\log 2\pi\):

    agent_2 agent_10 agent_1 Group
    \(\log\mu(u_i)\) when sampled −0.963 −1.083 −2.583 −4.629 (the sum)

    Ratio (C2). Some minibatches later the heads have moved a little: agent_2's quantity mean 0.30 → 0.36, agent_10's price mean 0.20 → 0.235, agent_1's price mean 0.50 → 0.53. Recomputed:

    agent_2 agent_10 agent_1 Group
    \(\log\pi_\theta(u_i)\) −0.869 −0.915 −2.563 −4.347
    \(\rho_i = e^{\log\pi_\theta - \log\mu}\) 1.099 1.182 1.020 $\rho = 1.099 \times 1.182 \times 1.020 = $ 1.324

    Clip (C3). RLlib clips the group's ratio, \(\rho = 1.324\), to \([0.8, 1.2]\). With \(\hat A = +0.8\):

    \[S = \min(1.324 \times 0.8,\ 1.2 \times 0.8) = \min(1.060,\ 0.960) = 0.960,\]

    the clipped branch: no gradient for any agent, although each agent alone (\(1.099\), \(1.182\), \(1.020\)) is still inside \([0.8, 1.2]\). Three agents that each moved a little count as one policy that moved too far. With \(\hat A = -0.8\) the unclipped branch is the smaller one (\(-1.060 < -0.960\)), so all three keep receiving gradient. The more agents, the sooner the product leaves the band. Clipping each agent's ratio separately instead, as MAPPO does, is the open choice recorded in training.md; the fixed code keeps RLlib's joint clip.

    Where the gradient goes (C8). Agent \(i\)'s network appears only in its own term of \(\log\pi = \sum_i \log\pi_i(u_i \mid o_i)\), so it receives only that term's gradient. On the first minibatch (\(\rho = 1\), unclipped), with \(\hat A = +0.8\), the push on each mean is \(\hat A \cdot \partial\log\pi / \partial m_k = \hat A\,(u_k - m_k)/\sigma_k^2\):

    agent_2 q agent_2 p agent_10 q agent_10 p agent_1 q agent_1 p
    \((u_k - m_k)/\sigma_k^2\) 1.649 −1.359 0.403 4.980 −2.984 0.738
    × \(\hat A = 0.8\) 1.319 −1.087 0.322 3.984 −2.387 0.590

    Each mean is pulled toward the action that was sampled, in proportion to how far the noise took it (\(\varepsilon_k / \sigma_k\)). agent_10's head gets only the two numbers in its columns, from the gradient of its own 2 outputs, and nothing from the other agents' actions; that is "decentralised execution" in the loss. In CTCE the same six numbers arrive, but all at one network that sees \(s\).

    In the original, these gradients went to a central actor and the acting heads got none (I-48).

    6. The settings we choose¶

    SETTINGS and MODEL, and what each changes in the cycle:

    Setting Value What it does in the cycle
    lr \(10^{-5}\) Adam moves each weight about \(10^{-5}\) per step (C10)
    gamma, lambda_ 0.99, 0.95 discount and GAE horizon, ≈ 17 steps (B4)
    num_epochs, minibatch_size 30, 128 with RLlib's 4000 steps per batch: 30 × ⌈4000/128⌉ ≈ 940 gradient steps per iteration
    clip_param 0.2 the band \([0.8, 1.2]\) of section 5
    entropy_coeff 0.01 the constant push to widen \(\sigma\) (C5)
    grad_clip 0.5 gradient norm capped at 0.5 (C9)
    use_kl_loss False the KL term is not computed at all (C6 disappears; fixes R-02)
    vf_share_layers False actor and critic have separate encoders: the value loss no longer trains the actor's layers (C8)
    SAC target_entropy "auto" \(-\dim A\) of each module: −2 per agent in DTDE, −6 for the group in CTCE and CTDE

    Steps per iteration. One PPO iteration samples 4000 environment steps. In CTCE and CTDE that is 4000 rows of the group; in DTDE 4000 rows for each of the three modules, 12 000 rows in total, but still 4000 environment steps. The budget counts environment steps, so all paradigms get the same amount of experience.

    7. Evaluating a trained policy¶

    Policy acts with the trained module outside RLlib, the same way RLlib does in training, but without noise.

    The action (act, L96-L111). The most likely action is the mean, then RLlib's own rescaling with the joint bounds, then the split:

    agent_2 agent_10 agent_1
    PPO, APPO: \(a = \text{rescale}(m)\) (1.50, 0.185) (−12.0, 0.230) (0.20, 0.275)
    SAC: \(a = \text{rescale}(\tanh m)\) (1.46, 0.185) (−10.74, 0.230) (0.199, 0.269)

    For example agent_10's quantity: \(-20 + (-0.60 + 1) \times 20 = -12.0\). SAC's actions are squashed by tanh, so its most likely action is \(\tanh m\); RLlib's own deterministic SAC action skips the tanh in Ray 2.51.1, so _deterministic (L39-L51) applies it. The original's inference used the mean without rescaling, so agent_10 would have bid −0.60 kWh (I-61).

    Baselines (L137-L165). "Random" draws each action uniformly within its bounds. "Zero" sends 0 in the market's units, clipped to the bounds: quantity 0, price \(\max(0, 0.05) = 0.05\) for every agent. It is not the network's 0, which would be the middle of the bounds (price 0.20).

    The team score (Episode, L172-L192). Every paradigm is scored in the original multi-agent environment, from the raw per-agent rewards. Over a 3-step episode:

    Step agent_2 agent_10 agent_1
    1 1.50 −0.50 0.25
    2 0.80 0.30 −0.10
    3 −0.20 1.10 0.40
    Return 2.10 0.90 0.55

    Team score \(= 2.10 + 0.90 + 0.55 = 3.55\), reported with the three returns. The same number for CTCE, CTDE and DTDE, computed the same way, so the nine combinations and the baselines can be ranked on one scale (I-73). Evaluation episodes use seeds from 1 000 000 on, which training never reaches (evaluation_seeds, L245-L249).

    Policy.values gives the critic's estimate of a state; in CTCE and CTDE that is the group's value, comparable to the team score, not to one agent's return.

    8. The run: budget, NaN stop, evaluation¶

    train repeats RLlib's iteration until the budget of environment steps is used up.

    Budget. budget = 100_000 with PPO's 4000 steps per iteration: 25 iterations, for every paradigm. With eval_every = 20_000, the policy and baselines are evaluated at 0 (untrained, together with random and zero), 20 000, 40 000, 60 000, 80 000 and at the end, 100 000 (L283-L302).

    NaN stop (_nonfinite, L194-L209). After each iteration the learner's results are searched for losses, entropies and the SAC temperature that are NaN or infinite. For

    {"group": {"policy_loss": nan, "vf_loss": 0.31, "entropy": inf,
           "num_env_steps_trained_throughput": nan}}

    it finds group/policy_loss and group/entropy and stops the run with an error, which run.json records. The throughput is skipped on purpose: RLlib reports it as NaN in the first iteration. The original carried on with zeroed gradients (R-02).
