    SimBench: the networks behind the Studio¶

    What SimBench is, how its codes and element names read, which grids and scenarios it has, and the study cases it defines. Every network on the Studio's Inspect page comes from here. The networks one by one: network catalogue · The time series of their loads and generators: profiles.

    Source of everything not measured in the Studio: the SimBench documentation, version 1.1.0 (Meinecke et al., University of Kassel, Fraunhofer IEE, RWTH Aachen, TU Dortmund), and the paper Meinecke et al. (2020), "SimBench—A Benchmark Dataset of Electric Power Systems to Compare Innovative Solutions Based on Power Flow Analysis", Energies 13(12), 3290. Section numbers like §4.2.3 refer to the documentation. Downloads: simbench.de.

    1. What SimBench is¶

    A benchmark dataset of German electricity grids from low to extra-high voltage, made so that methods can be compared on the same grids. It has:

    • Grids for four voltage levels (LV 0.4 kV, MV 20 kV, HV 110 kV, EHV 220/380 kV), each in several types, and combinations of two levels (§4.1).
    • Time series for one whole year, 2016, every 15 minutes, for every load, generator and storage unit (§3.5, §4.1.5). See profiles.
    • Three scenarios per grid: today and two future years with more PV, heat pumps, electric vehicles and storage (§3.6, §4.1.6).
    • Two switch variants per grid (§4.2.3).
    • Study cases: fixed scaling factors for the classic planning cases (§5.1).

    The grids are not real networks. They are built so that the use cases of grid planning and operation are covered, and checked against real grid data. The LV grids, for example, were generated from OpenStreetMap data for six representative German municipalities and compared with 180 real LV grids (§3.4, §4.1.4).

    The Studio reads SimBench through the Python package simbench, which gives each grid as a pandapower network.

    2. The SimBench code¶

    Every grid or grid combination has a code of six parts separated by - (§4.2.3, Table 4.9). Example: 1-MVLV-rural-2.107-0-sw.

    Part Meaning Values Example
    1 SimBench version 1 1
    2 Voltage level(s) LV, MV, HV, EHV, or two levels: MVLV, HVMV, EHVHV MVLV
    3 Grid type (of the higher level) LV: rural1, rural2, rural3, semiurb4, semiurb5, urban6; MV: rural, semiurb, urban, comm; HV: mixed, urban; EHV: mixed rural
    4 Which lower-level grid is modelled in detail (combinations only): one subnet number, or all empty for one level 2.107
    5 Scenario 0 today, 1 2024, 2 2034 ("heute", "morgen", "übermorgen") 0
    6 Switches sw all switches modelled, no_sw only open line and transformer switches sw

    So 1-LV-semiurb4--0-sw is the semi-urban LV grid 4 alone (part 4 empty), today, with switches.

    Switches. In sw every switch is a bus-to-bus switch, with extra auxiliary buses between the bus bar and each line or transformer. In no_sw buses joined by closed switches are merged: the same grid with fewer buses, often a faster power flow. The loads and generators are the same in both.

    Combinations. A grid of two levels models the higher grid in detail and either one chosen lower grid or all of them. For the MV-LV combinations SimBench preselected three LV grids at different places in the MV grid (e.g. 1.108, 2.107, 4.101), which keeps the number of codes manageable (§4.2.3).

    The Inspect page's filters follow the code: Voltage level is part 2, Type part 3, Scenario part 5, Switches part 6. The Studio's catalogue has 240 SimBench codes: the full "complete data" set and the four-level combination are left out because they are far larger than a local market needs.

    3. Element names: the subnet¶

    Every element has a subnet that says which grid it belongs to (§4.2.2, Table 4.8). That is why the Inspect page shows names like LV4.101 Load 1.

    Subnet Reads as
    HV2 HV grid of type 2 (HV and EHV subnets have two parts)
    MV3.201 MV grid of type 3, the first one (01) connected to an HV grid of type 2 (2xx)
    LV4.101 LV grid of type 4 (semi-urban 4), the first one connected to an MV grid of type 1

    The third part counts from 1 + 100 · (type of the higher grid). Buses on the border between two grids carry both subnets, joined by _. Equivalent elements, which stand in for a grid that is not modelled in detail, end in _eq.

    4. Voltage levels and grid types¶

    How many codes the Studio offers per level (all types × 3 scenarios × 2 switch variants, combinations × their lower grids):

    Level Codes Grid types Typical use
    LV 36 rural 1–3, semi-urban 4–5, urban 6 households, farms, small PV: local energy markets
    MV 24 rural, semi-urban, urban, commercial whole LV grids as one load, wind, biomass
    HV 12 mixed, urban regional networks
    EHV 6 mixed Germany's transmission grid
    MVLV 96 MV type × one or all LV grids an MV grid with LV grids in detail
    HVMV 48 HV type × one or all MV grids
    EHVHV 18 EHV × HV grids

    The six LV grids¶

    The grids a local energy market lives on (§4.1.4, Table 4.5). Each is radial and fed from the 20 kV grid through one MV/LV transformer.

    Grid Class it represents Transformer Feeders Nodes Consumers Load (total) DER (number, power) Cable Line length (total, longest)
    LV rural 1 rural 3 160 kVA 4 13 13 80 kW 4, 160.4 kW NAYY 4x150 0.56 km, 137 m
    LV rural 2 rural 2 250 kVA 4 96 99 202 kW 8, 145 kW NAYY 4x150 1.47 km, 75 m
    LV rural 3 rural 1 400 kVA 9 128 118 331 kW 17, 190 kW NAYY 4x150 2.35 km, 140 m
    LV semiurb 4 semi-urban 2 400 kVA 3 43 41 243 kW 1, 6.5 kW NAYY 4x150 0.75 km, 60 m
    LV semiurb 5 semi-urban 1 630 kVA 6 110 104 409 kW 9, 137 kW NAYY 4x240 1.79 km, 98 m
    LV urban 6 urban 630 kVA 7 58 111 441 kW 5, 57 kW NAYY 4x240 1.08 km, 78 m

    Values are scenario 0. At LV all generation is PV (no wind at this level). The consumers' rated power comes from the building floor area in OpenStreetMap (2 kW per average dwelling, varied by grid), and some values were raised by hand to load the grids more. Load types (household, commercial, farm) were assigned to the consumers by hand and at random. "LV rural 1" holds many farms, which is why its loads are larger than in real grids of its class.

    Cable data (Table 4.6): NAYY 4x150SE r = 0.2067 Ω/km, x = 0.0804 Ω/km, 270 A; NAYY 4x240SE r = 0.1267 Ω/km, x = 0.0798 Ω/km, 357 A.

    5. Scenarios¶

    Scenario Year What changes
    0 today (2016 data) the grids as built
    1 2024 more PV, plus heat pumps, electric vehicles and PV batteries
    2 2034 more of the same; some grids get new lines and buses to priority areas for wind and PV (MV, HV)

    How the future grids were made (§3.6): new generators and consumers were added following German forecasts (the grid development plan) until the grid shows problems, i.e. until about 15 % of its lines or nodes are overloaded or out of voltage limits in at least some of the grids. The scenarios are meant to be challenging, not to predict any one place.

    At LV, averaged over all six grids (§4.1.6):

    Scenario 1 (2024) Scenario 2 (2034)
    New PV, as a share of all PV plants 55 % 70 %
    Houses with an electric vehicle 4.5 % 19 %
    Houses with a heat pump 6 % 23 %

    In LV rural 3, scenario 1 mostly brings overvoltage (PV at noon), scenario 2 mostly undervoltage and overloaded lines and transformer (heat pumps and EVs). Scenario 0 has no heat pumps, EV charging or storage in any LV grid. The network catalogue shows the effect on every grid.

    6. Study cases¶

    Fixed snapshots for planning studies, as scaling factors of the rated load and generation (§5.1, Tables 5.1 and 5.2). The Studio works with the time series instead, but the cases are useful to judge a power flow: a network should pass hL and lPV.

    Case Situation Load Wind PV Other DER
    hL high load, low DER 1.00 0 0 0
    n1 as hL, with one element out (contingency) 1.00 0 0 0
    hW high load, very high wind 1.00 1.00 0.80 1.00
    hPV high load, very high PV 1.00 0.85 0.95 1.00
    lW low load, very high wind 0.10 (MV/LV), 0.25 (HV) 1.00 0.80 1.00
    lPV low load, very high PV 0.10 (MV/LV), 0.25 (HV) 0.85 0.95 1.00

    Slack and transformer voltage set points: in hL, n1, hW and hPV 1.025 pu (HV), 1.035 pu (MV), 0.965 pu (LV); in lW and lPV 1.015 pu (HV), 1.055 pu (MV). Load power factor cos φ = 0.93 (high-load cases) or 0.9 (low-load cases), underexcited. The cases ship with every network (StudyCases.csv, in pandapower net.loadcases).

    7. Data format¶

    SimBench's own format is a set of CSV tables, one per element type plus type tables (e.g. cable types) and profile tables (§4.3.1). Voltages and currents are three-phase and symmetrical, powers three-phase, 50 Hz. The pandapower networks the Studio uses are converted from these tables by the simbench package; their profiles are in net.profiles (tables load, renewables, powerplants, storage), linked to the elements by their profile column.
