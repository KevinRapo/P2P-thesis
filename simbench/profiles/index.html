    SimBench profiles: the time series of loads and generators¶

    What a SimBench profile is, how the Studio turns it into power, and where each class of profiles comes from: households, businesses, farms, heat pumps, electric vehicles, PV, wind, biomass, hydro, storage and whole grids. SimBench in general: overview · Which networks use which profiles: network catalogue.

    Section numbers like §3.5.2 refer to the SimBench documentation, version 1.1.0 (see the overview for the full reference). Counts, means and peaks in the tables are measured from the data set the Studio reads (scenario 0 unless stated).

    1. What a profile is¶

    A profile is a normalised time series: for every quarter hour of 2016 it gives a factor of an element's rated power. The element (a load, static generator or storage unit) names its profile in its profile column; many elements share one profile.

    \[ P_e(t) = p_{e} \cdot f_{\text{profile}(e)}(t), \qquad Q_e(t) = q_{e} \cdot f^{q}_{\text{profile}(e)}(t) \]

    with \(p_e\) the element's rated active power (p_mw) and \(q_e\) its rated reactive power (q_mvar).

    Property Value
    Year 2016, a leap year: 366 days
    Resolution 15 minutes: 96 values per day, 35,136 per year
    Scale factor of rated power; 1.0 = rated power
    Reactive power loads have a P and a Q profile (H0-A_pload, H0-A_qload); generators and storage have P only
    Tables load, renewables (PV, wind, biomass, hydro, grids), powerplants (EHV plants, imports), storage

    Things to know when reading the numbers:

    • Peaks are not always 1. Load profiles reach 1.0; PV profiles peak at most at 0.63 of rated power, because panels rarely deliver their rating.
    • Q factors can be negative: some measured loads feed reactive power back at times.
    • Rated power is not energy. A household of 3 kW with H0-A draws on average 0.19 × 3 kW ≈ 0.6 kW. The mean factors below show how far each class stays below its rating.
    • Time stamps. Measurements from other years (feed-in 2011/2012, hydro 2017, households 2010) were moved onto the 2016 calendar, with daylight saving time taken into account; the leap day was filled with the mean of the neighbouring same weekdays (§3.5.4).

    In the Studio¶

    The Inspect page shows profiles in two ways:

    • Factor (the Profiles section of a network, and the Profiles tab with SimBench's whole catalogue): the normalised values above.
    • kW ("Sum per profile" / "Per element"): factor × rated power of the elements that use the profile, as in the formula above.

    Resolutions: 15 min is the raw data; hourly is the mean of the four quarter hours of each hour (the market's default step, 24 per day); daily is the mean of all 96 values of a day.

    3. Accumulated consumers: households, businesses, farms¶

    Accumulated consumers are many similar customers that are planned with a standard load profile (SLP), the German utilities' typical-day curves: H0 households, G0–G6 businesses, L0–L2 farms, B0 band load (§3.5.1). SimBench replaces each SLP by a real measured time series that matches it, so the profiles keep the randomness of real customers.

    How the series were chosen:

    1. Data. Businesses and farms: 2,539 anonymised 15-minute measurements of 2016 (customers with a power meter, mostly under 100,000 kWh a year), after removing faulty files (~10 %) and series with cos φ < 0.8 (~14 %). Households: the IZES data set (HTW Berlin, 2010), resampled to 15 minutes by taking every 15th value instead of averaging, so the short-term peaks stay.
    2. Average week. Each series is averaged to one week (each weekday over the year, seasons ignored) and normalised to the week's mean.
    3. Matching. The distance to every SLP is the cumulative Euclidean distance (load and time) between the points of the two curves. A series is assigned to the SLP it is closest to, if it is not as close to another one.
    4. Size classes. By mean consumption per 15 minutes: 0–4 kWh for LV, 10–20 kWh for MV (318 series, 12.5 %), above 100 kWh for HV (17 series, 0.7 %).

    The suffix after the dash is the consumption level and size class: -A, -B, -C … are different low-consumption series of the same class (LV), -M medium (MV), -H high (HV).

    Households (H0)¶

    H0-A, H0-B, H0-C: the three household series closest to H0 (§4.1.5, Figure 4.15). H0-G and H0-L: households that match the business profile G6 and the farm profile L1 better than H0. They were kept on purpose, so the set covers the spread of real households: many real households use much more at weekends than H0 assumes. H0-H: an HV-size industrial customer whose shape resembles H0 (high base load, even rise over the week).

    Commercial (G0–G6)¶

    G for Gewerbe. SLP meaning: G0 business in general, G1 weekdays 8–18 h, G2 mainly evening, G3 continuous, G4 shops and hairdressers, G5 bakeries, G6 weekend business. Series: G0-A, G0-M, G1-A, G1-B, G1-C, G2-A, G3-A, G3-M, G3-H, G4-A, G4-B, G4-M, G4-H, G5-A, G6-A.

    G0 is itself a mix of the other G profiles, so no real customer is "G0"; a measured series is supplied anyway because grid operators often use G0 for all businesses. Most measured business series matched G1 (over 20 % of the data).

    Agricultural (L0–L2)¶

    L for Landwirtschaft: L0 farms in general, L1 farms with dairy, L2 other farms. Series: L0-A, L1-A, L2-A, L2-M. Farm profiles have an even demand over the week with high morning and evening peaks, which is also why many measured households matched them.

    Other (BL-H, WB-H)¶

    Two HV-size series with an almost constant demand ("band load"). They matched B0 and L2 only loosely and are listed as "other".

    4. Individual consumers: heat pumps and electric vehicles¶

    One device each, simulated bottom-up rather than measured (§3.5.2). Both use a fixed lagging power factor cos φ = 0.93, since the models give no reactive power.

    Heat pumps¶

    The electrical demand of a heat pump that heats a three-person single-family house in Hanover or Lübeck (heat demand of 2016 with 2011 weather, so it correlates with the PV and wind series).

    Name Heat source and operation
    Air_Parallel_1/2 Air source. Below −2 °C the pump and a backup heater run together
    Air_Alternative_1/2 Air source. Below −2 °C the pump stops and the heater covers everything
    Air_Semi-Parallel_1/2 Air source. Both run between −2 °C and −5 °C; below −5 °C only the heater
    Soil_Alternative_1/2 Ground source (probe), soil at a constant 7 °C. The documentation describes only parallel operation for ground-source pumps; the data names it "Alternative"

    _1 and _2 are the two cities. How the series behave:

    • Lockout periods. The utility blocks heat pumps every day 07:45–09:30, 11:30–12:30 and 16:45–19:00: the profile is 0 then, and catches up afterwards.
    • Storage. A hot-water tank (8.7 kWh) and a space-heating tank (11–14 kWh) are charged when either falls below 50 % and filled to 100 %; hot water first.
    • Efficiency. COP = 0.36 · Carnot COP, flow temperature 55 °C.
    • Size. Pumps of 3.8–4.9 kW with heaters of 0.5–10.7 kW; in the grids, heat pumps of 2–30 kW use these profiles (larger ones at MV).

    Electric vehicles¶

    Charging power at one charging point, from a bottom-up simulation of drivers (§3.5.2, §4.1.5): car model, battery (20–100 kWh) and consumption (15 or 25 kWh/100 km) drawn from the German fleet, measured charging curves of ten EV models, and trips from the survey Mobilität in Deutschland (number of trips, arrival times, purpose, distance, speed). A car charges with 80 % probability if it parks over three hours (20 % otherwise), always below 20 % state of charge.

    Name Charging point Power
    HLS_A_3.7, HLS_B_3.7, HLS_C_3.7 at home (Heimladestation) 3.7 kW
    HLS_A_11.0, HLS_B_11.0 at home 11 kW
    HLS_A_22.0 at home 22 kW
    APLS_A_3.7, APLS_B_3.7 at work (Arbeitsplatzladestation) 3.7 kW
    APLS_A_11.0, APLS_B_11.0 at work 11 kW
    APLS_A_22.0 at work 22 kW
    APLS_A_50.0 at work 50 kW
    APLS_0.1014, APLS_0.5202, APLS_0.033 aggregated workplace charging for MV grids (name: nominal power in MW) e.g. 101.4 kW, the sum of the LV workplace series; ×5 for large companies

    The single-point series are normalised to a maximum of 1. The aggregated ones are not, because all chargers never run at once. With a mean factor of 0.01, an EV charger draws its full power only for short times: these are the most "peaky" profiles in SimBench.

    5. Generation¶

    PV, wind, biomass, hydro¶

    PV, wind and biomass series were simulated with SIMONA, an agent-based grid simulation of the TU Dortmund, from weather data of the German Weather Service for 2011 and 2012 (§3.5.4). Plant sites follow the HV grids, so a wind farm in an HV grid gets the weather of its region. Hydro series are real, anonymised plant data of 2017. All are normalised to the plant's rating and have no reactive power: reactive power depends on how a plant is controlled, which SimBench leaves to the user (the study cases assume cos φ = 1).

    Name Site PV orientation
    PV1 Hanover (site 3) east
    PV2 Lübeck (site 1) east
    PV3 Hanover (site 1) south
    PV4 Hanover (site 2) south
    PV5 Lübeck (site 2) south
    PV6 Lübeck (site 3) south
    PV7 Hanover (site 2) west

    WP3, WP9, WP6 Baltic coast, North Sea coast, East Germany

    Hydro1 … Hydro3 run-of-river plants

    For a local market at LV, PV is the generation that matters: it is the only generation SimBench connects at LV. Its mean factor of 0.07 means a 10 kW system yields on average 0.7 kW, concentrated around noon and in summer.

    Power plants and imports¶

    Only in the EHV grids: pp_1 … pp_318, one profile per conventional power plant (many are constant), and imp0, imp1, generators at the border that stand for power from neighbouring countries.

    6. Storage¶

    SimBench models two kinds of batteries (§3.5.5, §4.1.5):

    • PV batteries that maximise self-consumption: the PV series is coupled to a household or business series; surplus PV charges the battery, a deficit discharges it. PV size = household energy × 0.00071 /h; the battery is large enough to store any surplus; charge and discharge efficiency 0.95; self-discharge 4 % per month.
    • Grid-beneficial batteries next to wind and PV plants that store the top 3 % of the plant's energy (a fixed power limit above which the feed-in is stored).

    The profile is the battery's power at its bus as if only the PV system and the battery were connected; the household's own load is a separate element. Only the batteries actually installed in a grid have a profile, named after the series they combine: Storage_PV3_H0-A is a battery with PV3 next to an H0-A household.

    Scenario 0 ships no storage profiles: batteries only appear in the future scenarios, which have 60 of them.

    7. Aggregated grids¶

    Where a network connects a whole grid of the level below as one element, that element follows the summed time series of the grid it stands for (§3.5.3, §4.1.5):

    • lv_…, mv_…, hv_… (bottom-up). A year-long power flow of each SimBench grid, recorded at its slack: lv_rural1 is the total load (or generation) of LV rural 1, used by an MV network's loads. Load versions have P and Q; generation versions P only. mv_add1, mv_add2 are further MV grids.
    • HS0 … HS27, HSexp0, HSexp1 (top-down). The EHV grid covers all of Germany, but only some HV grids are modelled. The rest come from Germany's total demand (ENTSO-E), split into households (by population, H0), farms (2 % of demand, by farmland, L0), businesses (by employees and area, G0) and industry and transport (the remainder), assigned to EHV/HV nodes by Voronoi areas and reduced to 28 representative series by k-means. exp marks the two series for nodes outside Germany. cos φ = 0.93.

    For a local market these matter only as the background of MV and higher networks: an MV load called lv_semiurb4 is a whole neighbourhood, not a prosumer.

    8. Where the data differs from the documentation¶

    Found while writing this page, by comparing the documentation (v1.1.0) with the profiles the simbench package ships. The data is what the Studio uses.

    Topic Documentation says Data has
    Ground-source heat pumps only parallel operation (§3.5.2) profiles named Soil_Alternative_1/2
    Wind profiles table of sites lists WP1 … WP11 (Table 4.7) 12 profiles, WP1 … WP12
    Feed-in scale "normalised to one" (§4.1.5) factors of rated power: PV peaks at 0.63, not 1
    Aggregated grid profiles "normalised to one" (§4.1.5) peaks of 0.95 (load) and 0.92 (generation)
    Storage profiles active and reactive power series (§4.1.5) active power only
    Storage profiles per scenario — none in scenario 0; 60 in scenarios 1 and 2

    The documentation's wording "normalised to one" most likely means "relative to the rated power", which the data follows; the other rows are open.
