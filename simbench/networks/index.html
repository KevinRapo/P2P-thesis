    Network catalogue: what each grid offers¶

    Every network the Studio's Inspect page offers, with the facts that matter when choosing one for a study: size, load, generation, PV penetration, how many buses could become agents, and which load and generation types it contains. Use it to find networks of interest before opening them in the Studio. What SimBench is and how its codes read: SimBench overview · The time series behind the loads and generators: profiles · Raw data: networks.json.

    1. How to read the tables¶

    All values come from the networks themselves, as the Studio loads them (generated by python docs/networks.py --collect; the tables are rewritten from the stored data by python docs/networks.py).

    246 networks (128 SimBench grid × scenario rows after merging the switch variants, 6 pandapower built-ins), from docs/simbench/networks.json.

    Column Meaning
    Network SimBench code without the switch suffix: 1-LV-rural1--0 stands for 1-LV-rural1--0-sw and 1-LV-rural1--0-no_sw, which have the same elements (the variants differ only in whether bus-bar switches are modelled). The number after -- is the scenario: 0 today, 1 and 2 future scenarios with more PV, heat pumps, EV charging and storage
    Load, Generation Sum of the rated power of all loads and static generators (SimBench's reference values; the profiles scale these over the year, factor ≤ 1)
    Gen / load, PV / load Rated generation (all, or PV only) as a share of rated load: a simple measure of penetration. Above 100 % the network can export at times
    Load buses Buses with at least one load: the most agents a one-agent-per-bus placement can have
    Prosumer buses Buses with a load and a generator: candidates for prosumer agents, together with the buses that have storage
    Ratings, Geodata Whether lines have real current ratings (loading checks) and real coordinates (map layout)
    Loads by type Number of loads per SimBench class (households, commercial, farms, heat pumps, EV charging, aggregated grids; see Inspect → Profiles)

    Rated values are not energy: a 3 kW household with the H0 profile uses far less than 3 kW on average. The Studio's Inspect page shows the profiles over time.

    2. Highlights¶

    What the tables below show, for choosing a network for a local-market study:

    • The Studio's default network has almost no local generation. 1-LV-semiurb4--0 (the network Inspect opens first) has one PV generator, 6.5 kW against 243 kW of load (3 %), on 1 of its 39 load buses. A peer-to-peer market there has little to trade unless the agents get catalogue generation profiles. Scenario 2 of the same grid raises PV to 51 % on 6 buses.
    • 1-LV-rural1 is the small, generation-rich grid: 15 buses, 13 load buses (mostly farms), PV at 200–572 % of the load. Good for quick tests with real prosumers, but it has at most 13 agents (8 prosumers).
    • 1-LV-rural3 has the most real prosumers at LV: 17 (scenario 0) to 27 (scenario 2) buses with load and PV out of 118, and in scenario 2 also 35 heat-pump and EV-charging loads and 16 storage units: the richest LV grid for heterogeneous agents.
    • Scenarios matter more than the grid choice suggests. Scenario 0 has no heat pumps, EV charging or storage in any LV grid; scenarios 1 and 2 add them. PV/load can fall from scenario 1 to 2 because the load grows (heat pumps, EVs) faster than the PV.
    • At LV, storage never shares a bus with PV. Every LV storage unit (scenarios 1 and 2) sits on a bus with only a load, so the LV bus kinds are load, load+pv and load+storage; no LV bus has load, PV and storage together. Each unit holds 2 h of its power. MV networks do have load+gen+storage buses. Participants are placed by these kinds (placement.place_all, placement.kind_counts).
    • MV networks are not prosumer markets. Their loads are mostly whole LV grids aggregated into one element (lv_… profiles), and their generation is wind, biomass, hydro and aggregated LV generation; PV of their own is 0–5 % of the load. Every bus there is a "prosumer bus", but each is a neighbourhood.
    • The combined networks (MVLV, HVMV, EHVHV, the "all" variants) are large: up to 10,458 buses and 14,589 loads. They are listed for completeness; they are far beyond what multi-agent RL can train on.
