## 2. The three paradigms now

| | Environment the algorithm sees | Modules | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| DTDE | the market as it is | one per agent, RLlib's default | own bill |
| CTDE | `CentralStateEnv`: agent i observes `[o_i, o_1 … o_N]` | one per agent; actor reads `o_i`, critic reads everything | own bill |
| CTCE | `GroupedEnv`: one agent, joined observations and actions | one, RLlib's default | the sum of all bills (social planner) |

**DTDE and CTDE now optimise the same thing** (each agent its own bill) and differ
only in what the critic sees, so a difference between them is the central critic's
effect. CTCE is a different question by design: what one controller of all
households would achieve. The paper's CTDE summed the rewards (a team objective);
that is changed here because the market's prosumers are self-interested.

What this fixes, compared with the grouped CTDE of `clean`:

- the PPO and APPO clip is per agent (it was on the product of all agents' ratios,
  which clips more and more as the number of agents grows);
- rewards, values and advantages are per agent (it was one team value);
- SAC's temperature and entropy target are per agent.

**Known deviation:** SAC's CTDE critic is `Q_i(s, a_i)`, the global state and the
agent's own action. MADDPG / MASAC also give it the other agents' actions; that
needs the joint action in each agent's training batch, which RLlib does not
provide. PPO and APPO are unaffected (their critic is a state value).

**Not done (not needed for correctness):** parameter sharing between similar agents,
value normalisation (PopArt), separate actor and critic optimisers.

**Settings, not issues:** library options that are simply set one way for now and
can be switched on in the configuration. They are kept out of the thesis.

- Population-based training (Ray Tune) is off: a run has fixed hyperparameters and
  one seed; tuning and seeds are loops around runs.
- PPO's value-loss cap (`vf_clip_param`) is 1e6 instead of RLlib's 10, which would
  stop the critic's gradient for large value errors; the environment scales
  rewards to returns of order 1-10.
- SAC's target entropy is RLlib's `"auto"`.
- The market runs use a first choice of hyperparameters (learning rate 3e-4, PPO
  batch of 100 days), not tuned.

## 3. The market as an environment

Full definition in the module docstring of
[market_env.py](../core/env/market_env.py). In short:

- **Agents:** one per scenario participant.
- **Episode:** one market day, drawn at each reset from the configured `days`. The
  day's end terminates the episode (the hour is in the observation, so no bootstrap
  past midnight is needed).
- **Observation** (16 per agent, scaled to about [-1, 1]): hour (sin, cos); own
  generation, demand, net; battery (has one, state of charge, room to charge and
  discharge); own reputation; last clearing price within the tariff range; last
  step's peer and DSO volumes, grid balance, own reward and own net trade.
- **Action** (2 per agent, in [-1, 1]): a signed quantity, as a share of what the
  agent may sell or buy this step (eq. 15a-b; a small dead zone means "no order"),
  and a price between the feed-in tariff and the utility price. The quantity is
  feasible by construction, so no action is ever rejected.
- **Reward:** the agent's net bill for the step, scaled:
  `(profit − p_utility · deferred_demand) / reward_scale`. Settlement leaves energy
  that no trade covers unpriced, so profit alone would make not buying free;
  untraded imports are therefore charged at the utility price and untraded exports
  earn nothing.
- **Baselines:** `zero_policy` (never trade) and `net_position_policy` (the
  market's own bidding rule: offer the surplus, ask for the deficit, battery first).

Choices made here that the papers leave open, with the reason:

| Choice | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | individual bill per agent | self-interested prosumers; CTCE keeps the welfare objective as the planner benchmark |
| Reward | net bill, not the paper's five-part weighted reward | the economic outcome itself; no hand-tuned weights; the paper's reward has three inconsistent versions ([papers.md](validation/papers.md)) |
| Action | 2 numbers: signed share of the feasible quantity, price in [FiT, utility] | no infeasible or rejected orders (I-10, I-39); no discrete parts decoded from continuous numbers; prices outside the tariffs are dominated |
| Partner preference β | not learned | the stage-1 preference adds a discrete choice per step; left for later |
| Scaling | fixed scales in the environment | the same in training and evaluation; returns of order 1-10 |
| Day end | termination | a market day settles; the hour is observed |
| Days | drawn from a list per episode | train on some days, evaluate on held-out ones (I-72) |

## 4. How to use it

```python
from core.env import MarketEnv, net_position_policy, zero_policy
from core.rl import Run, evaluate, load_policy, train

config = {"scenario": "scenarios/validate_market.json",
          "days": [f"2016-06-{d:02d}" for d in range(1, 31)]}
record = train(Run("ppo", "ctde", MarketEnv, config, budget=24_000, eval_every=4_800,
                   training={"lr": 3e-4, "train_batch_size_per_learner": 2400,
                             "minibatch_size": 240, "num_epochs": 10}))

held_out = {"scenario": config["scenario"], "days": [f"2016-07-{d:02d}" for d in range(1, 15)]}
env = MarketEnv(held_out)
policies = {"trained": load_policy(record["out_dir"])[0],
            "zero": zero_policy(env), "net_position": net_position_policy(env)}
evaluate(MarketEnv, held_out, policies, seeds=list(range(2000, 2014)))
```

Run from the worktree root (the RL code is imported as `core.rl`, the market as
`core.*`). Each run writes `run.json`, `metrics.csv`, `evaluation.csv` and a
checkpoint to `runs/` (not in git).

### Running experiments

For more than one run, or one that should outlive the terminal, describe the runs
in `experiments/<name>.json` (algorithms x paradigms x seeds x variants, budget,
train and test days; see `core/runner/spec.py` for the fields) and use the runner:

```
python -m core.runner start experiments/smoke.json   # returns at once
python -m core.runner status                          # one line per experiment
python -m core.runner status smoke                    # table: state, progress, ETA, scores
python -m core.runner stop smoke [run]                # kill the worker and jobs (or one run)
```

A detached worker keeps up to `max_parallel` runs training; each run ends with
`heldout.csv` (trained policy and baselines on the test days). Everything is in
files under `runs/<name>/`, so `status` works from any terminal, and the Studio can
read the same files. `start` on an existing experiment resumes it: completed runs
stay, failed, stopped and lost runs (process gone) start again.

## 5. Validation

### Probes (RL layer, before the market)

The five probe environments, all nine combinations, fast settings (2 × 64 network,
learning rate 1e-3). A run passes when every value and action is within tolerance
of the exact answer; DTDE and CTDE values are checked per agent, CTCE's against the
group sum; SAC's discount-probe value includes its entropy bonus.

| | CTCE | CTDE | DTDE |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPO | 1-5 pass | 1-5 pass | 1-5 pass (probe 4: 1.04 on seed 0, a marginal miss; passes at twice the budget, 0.76, and on seed 1, 0.37) |
| APPO | 1-5 pass | 1-5 pass | 1-5 pass |
| SAC | 1-2 pass; 3-5 pass at three times the budget | same | same |

SAC's action probes need more steps in every paradigm, DTDE included (RLlib's own
module): its entropy bonus and tanh squashing pull the actions toward 0 until the
temperature has fallen. That is SAC's behaviour, not a paradigm defect.

### Market

Scenario `scenarios/validate_market.json` (5 households on
`1-LV-rural1--0-sw`, two with batteries and PV, two pure consumers). Trained on
the 30 days of June 2016, 24,000 environment steps (1,000 days); evaluated on 14
held-out days (1-14 July 2016) with the most likely action. Team return = sum of
the five agents' scaled daily bills (higher is better).

| Policy | Steps (days) | CTCE | CTDE | DTDE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero (never trade) | – | −12.64 | −12.64 | −12.64 |
| PPO | 24,000 (1,000) | −11.43 | −10.30 | −9.04 |
| APPO | 24,000 (1,000) | −12.43 | −10.51 | −10.72 |
| SAC | 6,000 (250) | −11.26 | −12.35 | −12.50 |
| net position (the market's rule) | – | −8.47 | −8.47 | −8.47 |

- **Every combination learns something:** all nine end above never trading on days
  they were not trained on. PPO and APPO in CTDE and DTDE are clearly better; APPO-CTCE,
  SAC-CTDE and SAC-DTDE only just (they mostly learned to trade P1's surplus).
- **None beats the market's own bidding rule yet,** and no training curve had
  levelled off: PPO-DTDE went from −12.0 to −7.4 on training days. These are single
  runs, one seed, short budgets; the ordering between paradigms is not a result.
- **SAC had a quarter of the budget**: each SAC step costs far more (a gradient update
  per sampled batch), so 6,000 steps took about 40 minutes per paradigm, as long as
  24,000 PPO steps. Its runs say "it learns", not how it compares.
- Wall time per run: PPO and APPO about 30-35 min, SAC 15-40 min, three in parallel.
