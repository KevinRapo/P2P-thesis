# marlem-v2: PPO with the paper's mixed market action

> Branch `marlem-v2` (worktree `workspace/marlem-v2`): the market on SimBench
> networks with an AC power flow or the paper's graph model, RLlib's PPO in the
> three paradigms with the paper's four action components as genuine categorical
> and continuous parts, the runner, SLURM submission, synchronisation, results and
> the Studio. These pages are not published (see [About](about.md#publishing)).

## 1. Scope

| Part | What this branch does |
|---|---|
| Algorithm | PPO only (RLlib's loss, modules and distributions) |
| Paradigms | CTCE, CTDE, DTDE |
| Market action | the four parts of [P1] eq. 2: side and partner categorical, quantity and price continuous |
| Partner β | learned, passed to the order as chosen |
| Observation | the paper's 42 signals and the peers' reputations (46 with five agents; [Observation](rl/observation.md)) |
| Scales | resolved on the training days, recorded, reused everywhere |
| Grid | one scenario setting selects the power flow or the graph model ([grid methods](experiments/grid-methods.md)) |

Not in scope: other algorithms, forecasts in the observation, recurrent policies,
a new grid-distance model, the original's blockchain-style trade ledger (it changes
no market result) and an autoregressive policy. The policy is **factorised** over
the action parts (one distribution per part, sampled independently given the
observation): the partner is not chosen conditionally on the sampled side or price.
Multi-node learners and other training backends are not needed and not added.

### The market as an environment

The full definition is in the module docstring of
[market_env.py](../core/env/market_env.py). In short:

- **Agents:** one per scenario participant.
- **Episode:** one market day by default, drawn at each reset from the training
  days, or a window of `episode_days` days. The window's end terminates the episode
  unless `truncate_episode` is set.
- **Action and observation:** sections [2](#2-the-mixed-action) and
  [3](#3-observation).
- **Reward:** the agent's net bill for the step, scaled:
  `(profit − p_utility · deferred_demand) / reward_scale`. Settlement leaves energy
  that no trade covers unpriced, so profit alone would make not buying free;
  untraded imports are therefore charged at the utility price and untraded exports
  earn nothing.
- **Baselines:** `zero_policy` (never trade) and `net_position_policy` (the
  market's own bidding rule: offer the surplus, ask for the deficit, battery first).

Choices made here that the papers leave open, with the reason:

| Choice | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | individual bill per agent | self-interested prosumers; CTCE keeps the welfare objective as the planner benchmark |
| Reward | net bill, not the paper's five-part weighted reward | the economic outcome itself; no hand-tuned weights; the paper's reward has three inconsistent versions ([papers.md](validation/papers.md)) |
| Action | the quantity as a share of the feasible bound, the price in [FiT, utility] | no infeasible or rejected orders (E-04, E-05); prices outside the tariffs are dominated |
| Scaling | fixed scales in the environment | the same in training and evaluation; returns of order 1-10 |
| Day end | termination by default; `truncate_episode` and `terminal_value` as options | a market day settles and the hour is observed; the alternatives are an experiment (RP-1f) |
| Days | drawn from a list per episode | train on some days, evaluate on held-out periods (L-17) |

## 2. The mixed action

[P1] eq. 2 defines the action as $a_i = \langle p_{\text{bid}}, q_{\text{bid}}, \alpha, \beta \rangle$.
V2 gives each agent a `Dict` action with these four parts
([market_env.py](../core/env/market_env.py)):

| Part | Space | Paper | Encoding chosen here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `side` | `Discrete(2)` | α, order type, categorical | 0 sell, 1 buy |
| `quantity` | `Box([-1, 1])` | $q_{\text{bid}}$, continuous | share $s = (q + 1) / 2$ of the agent's eq. 15a-b bound for the chosen side; **no order** when $s < 0.05$; above it the share is rescaled to $(0, 1]$ |
| `price` | `Box([-1, 1])` | $p_{\text{bid}}$, continuous | $p = p_{\text{FiT}} + \frac{p + 1}{2}\,(p_{\text{utility}} - p_{\text{FiT}})$ |
| `partner` | `Discrete(N + 1)` | β, another agent, the DSO or none | index into the agent's candidates: the $N - 1$ other participants in scenario order, then the DSO, then "no preference" |

What is the paper's: the four parts and their kinds. What is an implementation
choice: the quantity as a share of the eq. 15 bound (every order is feasible, so
none is rejected, E-04, E-05), the no-order threshold (an explicit encoding
decision: the paper has no "no order" action), the price range between the two
tariffs, and the candidate order. The original code decoded all four parts from
one continuous `Box` (`int(β)`, `α > 0.5`); V2 does not decode anything
categorical from a continuous number.

The chosen partner goes into `OrderIntent.partner_id` unchanged, "no preference"
(None) included. The scenario's rule-based partner choice
(`market.partner_preference`, [`choose_partner`](../core/market/agent/der.py))
only fills in orders the market's own bidding rule makes; it never replaces an RL
agent's choice. Partner choices are not masked, and certainly not with the other
agents' simultaneous actions, which are not known when the agent acts.

The clearing ([clearing.py](../core/market/clearing.py)) has three stages:

1. **Stage 1, preferences:** a buyer and a seller that named each other trade
   first, at the mechanism's price, when bid ≥ ask.
2. **Stage 2, double auction:** every order still open, except those naming the
   DSO.
3. **Stage 3, DSO:** whatever is left, and the orders naming the DSO.

So a choice is *compatible* (mutual, opposite sides, prices cross: stage-1
trade), *incompatible* (mutual but bid < ask: no stage-1 trade), or *one-sided*
(the partner named someone else or nobody: the order goes to the auction).
[test_partner_choice.py](../tests/market/test_partner_choice.py) plays each.

## 3. Observation

Each agent observes the paper's 42 signals ([P1] Table 2): 16 market signals, its
own 16 agent signals and the 10 implicit-cooperation KPIs, computed in
[`signals.py`](../core/market/signals.py) and scaled by `MarketEnv`. Then come
the other agents' reputations (eq. 12), which [P1] adds when partner preference is
on, as the last step left them. Every entry, its definition and its scaling are in
[Observation](rl/observation.md).

The reputation slots follow the agent's candidate order (slot k is its k-th peer
candidate), so the observation and the `partner` part line up. The grid distances
to the candidates are not observed: they are fixed by the network, so the policy
can learn them from the slot. Not observed: the other agents' profiles, batteries,
bills and their orders of the current step (submitted at the same time, seen by
nobody before clearing). The same local observation is used in DTDE and CTDE;
CTDE's critic additionally reads every agent's observation (the global state), the
actor does not.

## 4. The paradigms with a mixed action

| Paradigm | Actor input | Critic input | Objective | PPO ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DTDE | own observation | own observation | own reward | one per agent, of its whole action |
| CTDE | own observation | own + every agent's observation | own reward | one per agent, of its whole action |
| CTCE | joint observation | joint observation | sum of rewards | one, of the joint action |

- DTDE and CTDE keep one module, one critic and one optimiser per agent
  ([config.py](../core/rl/config.py), [ctde.py](../core/rl/ctde.py)).
  Each module's distribution is RLlib's composite distribution over the agent's
  `Dict` (a categorical per discrete part, a Gaussian per continuous part); its
  log-probability is the sum over the parts, so PPO's ratio and clip apply to the
  agent's complete action.
- CTCE's `CTCEEnv` ([ctce.py](../core/rl/ctce.py)) keeps every agent's
  action space as it is, as one `Tuple` in agent order:
  one composite distribution for the group, one ratio for the joint controller.
- Evaluation and baselines ([policy.py](../core/rl/policy.py)) handle any such
  space: `to_env` applies RLlib's own `unsquash_action` (continuous parts only)
  and returns categorical parts as indices; without exploration each part takes
  its most likely value (a Gaussian's mean, a categorical's mode). Agent order is
  kept through grouping, decoding and checkpoint restoration (modules are declared
  in agent order; `ungroup` splits the `Tuple` by position).

**DTDE and CTDE optimise the same thing** (each agent its own bill) and differ only
in what the critic sees, so a difference between them is the central critic's
effect. CTCE is a different question by design: what one controller of all
households would achieve. The paper's CTDE summed the rewards (a team objective);
that is changed here because the market's prosumers are self-interested.

**Not done (not needed for correctness):** parameter sharing between similar
agents, value normalisation (PopArt), separate actor and critic optimisers.

**Settings, not issues:** library options set one way for now and switchable in the
configuration ([config.py](../core/rl/config.py)): population-based training is off
(a run has fixed hyperparameters and one seed; tuning and seeds are loops around
runs), and PPO's value-loss cap is raised, since RLlib's 10 would stop the critic's
gradient for large value errors.

## 5. The run contract

**Scales.** `MarketEnv` computes the energy scale E from its days and the reward
scale from E. A held-out environment built from the test days would compute a
different E, and observations and rewards would mean different things in training
and in the held-out evaluation. So the scales are resolved once per run
(`MarketEnv.resolved_config`, [train.py](../core/rl/train.py)), trains and
evaluates with them, records them in `run.json`, and the runner's held-out
evaluation reuses them ([`heldout_env_config`](../core/runner/launch.py)); a
restored policy is built from the same record. Monetary and physical results
(`profit`, `bill`, kWh, grid amounts) are reported in the infos and tables
independently of the scaled return.

**Run record (`run.json`).** `env_config` (resolved, with the
scales) and `env_config_given`; `env_contract` (observation names, action parts
and their encoding, the partner mapping per agent, the scales); `agents` and each agent's observation and action `spaces`;
`settings_effective` (every PPO setting with the value it ran with, given or
RLlib's default, and the rollout fragment length); `model_effective` (the full
network config); with the existing seed, `PYTHONHASHSEED`, versions and git
commit.

**Two evaluations.** `evaluation*.csv` is the *periodic* evaluation during
training: the trained policy and baselines (random, zero, net position) on the
training configuration, i.e. the training days, with seeds training does not use.
It shows whether the policy learns; it is not held out. `heldout*.csv` (the
runner, after training) plays every test period day by day, the battery carried
over within a period: the genuine held-out evaluation. The Studio's Results page
labels them so.

## 6. Studio, runner and SLURM

In Python, from the `marlem-v2` root (the RL code is imported as `core.rl`, the
market as `core.*`):

```python
from core.env import MarketEnv, net_position_policy, zero_policy
from core.rl import Run, evaluation, load_policy, train

config = {"scenario": "config/scenarios/validate_market.json",
          "days": [f"2016-06-{d:02d}" for d in range(1, 31)]}
record = train(Run("ppo", "ctde", MarketEnv, config, budget=24_000, eval_every=4_800))

held_out = {"scenario": config["scenario"], "days": [f"2016-07-{d:02d}" for d in range(1, 15)]}
env = MarketEnv(held_out)
policies = {"trained": load_policy(record["out_dir"])[0],
            "zero": zero_policy(env), "net_position": net_position_policy(env)}
days = held_out["days"]  # every test day once
found = evaluation(MarketEnv, held_out, policies, seeds=list(range(2000, 2014)),
                   options=[{"day": d} for d in days])
found.summary, found.episodes, found.agents  # means; per day; per day and participant
```

Each run writes `run.json`, `metrics.csv`, `evaluation.csv` (with its per-episode
and per-agent tables) and a checkpoint to `runs/` (not in git).

For more than one run, or one that should outlive the terminal, describe the runs
in `config/experiments/<name>.json` (algorithms × paradigms × seeds × variants,
budget, train days and test periods; every setting is in [Config](config/reference.md))
and use the runner. It keeps everything in files under `runs/<name>/`, so the CLI,
Python (`core.runner.targets`) and the Studio see the same state. Each run ends
with `heldout.csv` (trained policy and baselines on the test periods), with per-day
and per-participant tables next to it; the Studio's Results page and
`notebooks/runner/results.ipynb` compare runs from them. The `test` profile is the
smallest version of the same experiment, to check the pipeline before spending
cluster time. The workflow, the readiness check and restarting are in
[Training experiments](hpc/experiments.md), what each setting controls in
[Resources and parallelism](hpc/resources.md).

```powershell
python core/rl/scripts/probe_ppo.py
python -m pytest tests/market
python -m pytest tests/rl
python -m pytest tests/runner
python -m core.runner start config/experiments/market_ppo.json --profile test
python -m core.runner check config/experiments/market_ppo.json --target taltech
python -m core.runner start config/experiments/market_ppo.json --profile test --target taltech
python -m core.runner status market_ppo--test --target taltech
python -m core.runner sync market_ppo--test --target taltech
```

- One SLURM array task per run, the environment runners inside each run
  (`num_env_runners`); start, status, stop, restart and sync from the CLI or the
  Studio.
- The Studio's Experiments page edits an experiment's JSON, with fields for the
  paradigms, seeds, budget, PPO settings (learning rate, batch, minibatch, epochs,
  entropy, clip, γ), environment runners, runs in parallel, torch threads and SLURM
  resources; each rewrites its setting in the JSON editor, which stays what starts.
- The Results page shows every test day step by step: each participant's side, order,
  named partner, whether the choice was mutual and its stage-1 energy, and the step's
  stage-1 volume next to the P2P and DSO volumes.
- The cluster's checkout is `~/marlem-v2` (branch `marlem-v2`), with its own `runs/`;
  on this PC the synced results are in `runs/@taltech/`. Account and cluster settings
  are in `.env`.

### Reproducibility

The same seed repeats a run on one machine
([test_7_same_seed_same_run.py](../tests/rl/test_7_same_seed_same_run.py)). That
takes two things:

| Cause of a difference | Effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| RLlib given the per-agent modules as a set | Built, and given their initial weights, in an order that changes with every process | A list in agent order ([config.py](../core/rl/config.py)) |
| RLlib keeps agent ids in sets while sampling and batching | Python's per-process hash seed changes multi-agent runs | `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` in every runner job, local and SLURM ([launch.py](../core/runner/launch.py)); `run.json` records it |

Across machines, floating-point rounding differs slightly between CPUs and
libraries and training amplifies it, so identical results are expected on one
machine and statistically equivalent ones across machines. Runs started from a
notebook do not fix the hash seed unless the notebook sets `PYTHONHASHSEED` for the
processes it starts.

## 8. Open points

- Training budgets and PPO settings for the mixed action are a first choice, not
  tuned. [market_ppo.json](../config/experiments/market_ppo.json) still gives its own
  batch of 2,400 and minibatch of 240 over the settings chosen on the probes (RP-6b:
  learning rate 3e-4, batch 500, minibatch 64, 10 epochs).
- The factorised policy cannot express "name this partner only when buying";
  an autoregressive or partner-conditioned policy would need a custom
  distribution (out of scope).
- The no-order threshold and the quantity share are encoding choices; whether
  "no order" should be a third `side` category instead is open.
- The observation ([Observation](rl/observation.md)): the KPIs are taken over the
  day so far (the papers give no window, E-10); a signal with nothing to measure
  enters as 0, which for the local price and its advantage is also a real value
  (E-07); generation, demand and what remains of them are the true values, without
  the paper's forecast error (E-05).
- Ray's start-up occasionally fails when memory is short (several runs in parallel
  each start their own Ray); run fewer in parallel if it happens.
